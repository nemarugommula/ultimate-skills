Phase 8 · Recap

Recap: Audio, speech & multimodal

Sound becomes a picture or a stream of tokens; images become a few hundred visual words; and then it's all transformers. Here's the phase on one page, a diagnosis game, a voice assistant to design yourself, and the boss quiz.

🧾 Cheat sheet

🌈

8.1 Spectrograms

16 kHz, 25 ms window, 10 ms hop → 100 frames/s × 80 mel rows, then log. Max frequency = sample rate ÷ 2.

The STFT turns sound into an image (time × pitch). Longer windows: sharper pitch, blurrier time. Mel matches pitch perception, log matches loudness. SpecAugment masks bands and frames.

🧰 torch.stft, WhisperFeatureExtractor, torchaudio MelSpectrogram, librosa

📝

8.2 Speech recognition

CTC: merge repeats, drop blanks (blank between doubles). WER = (S + D + I) ÷ N, after normalization.

CTC sums over all alignments, fast and streamable; transducers add output dependence; Whisper is an encoder–decoder on 30 s log-mel windows, trained on 680,000 hours, and can hallucinate in silence.

🧰 nn.CTCLoss, Wav2Vec2ForCTC, Whisper pipeline, Open ASR Leaderboard

🗣️

8.3 Text-to-speech

Text → phonemes → mel → vocoder → waveform. Speed ≈ sequential steps per second of audio; RTF < 1 is real time.

Tacotron 2 (attention, slow, can skip words) → FastSpeech 2 (duration predictor, parallel) + HiFi-GAN (parallel vocoder); VITS end to end; VALL-E clones from 3 s with codec tokens.

🧰 Kokoro, F5-TTS, CosyVoice 2, Chatterbox; text-to-audio pipeline

🎛️

8.4 Audio tokens

Tokens/s = frames/s × codebooks. EnCodec 6 kbps: 600/s. Mimi: 100/s (5 min = 30,000).

RVQ quantizes leftovers stage by stage; drop stages to cut bitrate. Semantic tokens say what, acoustic tokens say how. Audio encoder + projector (Qwen2-Audio) or full duplex (Moshi, 160 ms).

🧰 EncodecModel, MimiModel, Qwen2Audio, kyutai moshi

🖼️

8.5 Vision-language models

Tokens = (side ÷ patch)² ÷ merge. 336 px, patch 14 → 576. Attention cost ∝ tokens².

Encoder → connector → LLM: MLP projector (LLaVA), Q-Former (32), resampler + cross-attention (Flamingo, 64), tiles or native resolution with 2×2 merge, early fusion (Chameleon). POPE polls for hallucinated objects.

🧰 AutoModelForImageTextToText, LLaVA, Qwen2-VL (min/max pixels)

🗣️ Teach it back

Answer out loud, like in an interview: “A user says ‘what's in this photo, and how long do I bake it?’ to a voice assistant and holds up a recipe card. Walk me through every model and representation involved, from the microphone to the voice that answers, with rough numbers. Then: the answer mentions an ingredient that isn't on the card. Where could that have come from?”

A strong answer covers: 16 kHz audio → log-mel (100 × 80 per second) → streaming ASR (CTC or transducer for speed, Whisper-style for accuracy) with VAD; the photo resized or tiled → ViT patches → projector → a few hundred to a few thousand image tokens in the LLM's context; the LLM answers in text; TTS (duration-based acoustic model + parallel vocoder, or a streaming codec LM) speaks sentence by sentence. Or a speech-to-speech model on codec tokens. Then the failure: ASR mishearing (check the transcript), the image too downscaled to read (resolution), or VLM object hallucination from the language prior (POPE-style), and how to tell them apart from logs.

🏗️ Build it yourself: a kitchen voice assistant

✏️ The problem

A smart-speaker start-up wants a hands-free kitchen assistant for 1,000 pilot households. Users talk to it while cooking (extractor fan on, pans sizzling), set timers, ask follow-up questions, and sometimes hold up a recipe card or a phone photo to a small camera: “how long do I bake this?”. Expect about 20 requests per household per day, typically a 4-second question and a 10-second spoken answer. Target: the first sound of the answer within about 1 second of the user finishing. Users must be able to interrupt (“stop!”).

Sketch the pipeline (ASR choice, VLM for the photo, TTS), the latency budget, tokens per request and a rough monthly cost, then open the reveal.

Reveal: one good answer

Pipeline

  1. On device: wake word and VAD on a small log-mel CNN (8.1), so audio only leaves the house after the wake word. Barge-in: keep VAD running while the speaker plays, and stop playback when the user talks (8.4).
  2. ASR: a streaming CTC or transducer model (8.2) for low latency and few hallucinations in silence; fine-tune or augment with kitchen noise (SpecAugment plus noise mixing). A Whisper-style model is a fine second pass for logging, not for the live path.
  3. Photo: a VLM with native resolution or tiling (8.5), capped. A 4,032 × 3,024 phone photo at Qwen2-VL-style native resolution would be 108 × 144 = 15,552 tokens; capping at 1,024 merged tokens resizes it to 1,008 × 756 → 972 tokens, still enough for printed recipe text. Crop to the card if a detector finds it.
  4. LLM: text in, text out, with timers and recipe lookup as tools. Stream tokens.
  5. TTS: a parallel or streaming model (8.3), synthesizing sentence by sentence, so the first sentence plays while the rest is generated. One house voice, no cloning of family members' voices without explicit consent; watermark output.

Latency budget (assumed numbers; measure your own)

  • End-of-turn detection 300–500 ms (the biggest single item; tune it, don't zero it) + streaming ASR final 100–200 ms + network 50–100 ms + LLM time to first token 300–500 ms + first sentence and TTS first audio 150–250 ms ≈ 0.9–1.5 s. Use the budget playground in AI Engineer 8.4.
  • A speech-to-speech model (Moshi-style, 8.4) could get under 300 ms, at the cost of control, tool use and an auditable transcript. For a pilot, the cascade is easier to debug.

Tokens and cost (illustrative prices)

  • Per request: ~500 tokens of system prompt and tools, ~30 tokens of transcript, ~200 of history → about 700 input tokens; if 10% of requests include a photo, add 972 × 0.1 ≈ 97 → ≈ 800 input, ≈ 150 output (a 10-second spoken answer is roughly 25–30 words).
  • 1,000 households × 20 = 20,000 requests a day. At an illustrative $0.50 per million input tokens and $2 per million output: 20,000 × (800 × 0.5 + 150 × 2) ÷ 10⁶ = $14 a day ≈ $420 a month for the LLM, before ASR and TTS (about 22 hours of audio in and 56 hours out per day).
  • If the audio went straight into an audio LLM as Mimi-style tokens instead: a 4-second question is 400 tokens, a 10-second answer is 1,000 output tokens, roughly 7× the text output. That's why cascades stay cheaper per request.

Classic mistakes

  • Feeding 44.1 kHz microphone audio to a 16 kHz model without resampling (8.1).
  • Using an encoder–decoder ASR on long silent stretches and getting invented sentences (8.2): VAD first.
  • Squashing a recipe photo to 336 px so oven temperatures become unreadable (8.5); or the opposite, letting 4K photos eat 15k tokens each.
  • Waiting for the full answer before starting TTS; an agent that can't be interrupted (8.4).
  • Shipping without a WER and hallucination eval on kitchen audio, normalized the same way every time.

🏆 Boss quiz

1. A 2-minute voice note goes into an audio LLM as Mimi tokens (12.5 frames per second, 8 codebooks). How many tokens does it use?
12.5 × 8 = 100 tokens a second; 120 s × 100 = 12,000. With EnCodec at 6 kbps (75 × 8 = 600 a second) it would be 72,000. Frame rate × codebooks is the whole calculation (Lesson 8.4).
2. A CTC model outputs the frames _ a a _ a b b _. What text is decoded?
Merge repeats: _ a _ a b _ (the two a-runs stay separate because a blank sits between them). Drop blanks: aab. The blank is what lets CTC write doubled letters (Lesson 8.2).
3. A team's TTS is natural on short prompts, but on long paragraphs it sometimes repeats a phrase and never stops. They also need a “speak 20% faster” setting. What should they switch to?
Repeats and endless babble are autoregressive alignment failures; explicit durations remove them and make speed control trivial (scale every duration by 1 ÷ 1.2). A bigger AR model fails the same way, and the codec only affects quality (Lesson 8.3).
4. LLaVA-NeXT-style AnyRes encodes a 672 × 672 image as a 2 × 2 grid of 336-px tiles plus one downscaled thumbnail, each 576 tokens. How many image tokens?
4 tiles × 576 = 2,304, plus the 576-token thumbnail = 2,880, five times LLaVA-1.5's 576 for four times the pixels. More pixels read means more tokens paid (Lesson 8.5).
5. You need on-device dictation that shows words while the user is still talking and must never invent sentences during pauses. Which ASR design fits best?
Transducers and CTC emit text frame by frame as audio arrives, and their outputs are tied to audio frames, so long invented passages are unlikely. Whisper processes 30-second windows with an autoregressive decoder (slower, and known to hallucinate in silence) (Lesson 8.2).
