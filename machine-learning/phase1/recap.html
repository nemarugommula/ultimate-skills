Phase 1 · Recap

Recap: Data & features

How to look at a table before modelling, fill its holes, tame its spikes, turn its words into numbers, put its columns on one ruler, build the features that carry the signal, and wrap it all in one leak-proof pipeline. Here's the phase on one page, a diagnosis game, a problem to design yourself, and Lab 1.

🧾 Cheat sheet

🔍

1.1 EDA

Plot before you trust a summary (Anscombe: four datasets, one correlation, 0.816). Beat the majority baseline. “Would I know this at prediction time?”

Shape, types, missing values, describe, the target's balance, histograms, group-bys. A feature that's too good is usually leakage (retention offer: 89% vs 5% churn).

🧰 pandas describe / value_counts / groupby / corr, seaborn, ydata-profiling

🕳️

1.2 Missing & outliers

MCAR / MAR / MNAR decide the fix. Median + missing indicator is a strong default. IQR rule over z-scores (masking).

Dropping rows loses data fast (0.97¹² ≈ 69% survive) and biases; mean filling shrinks spread; model imputation fixes MAR. One typo can flip a least-squares slope; Huber and RobustScaler resist it.

🧰 SimpleImputer(add_indicator), KNNImputer, IterativeImputer, HistGradientBoosting (NaN native), RobustScaler, HuberRegressor

🏷️

1.3 Encoding

Few categories → one-hot. Real order or trees → ordinal. Many → hashing or target encoding, smoothed and cross-fitted.

Naive target encoding on an ID column: train AUC 0.95, test 0.57. Cross-fitting makes the train score honest. Pool rare categories; plan for unseen ones.

🧰 OneHotEncoder(min_frequency, handle_unknown), OrdinalEncoder, TargetEncoder, FeatureHasher, CatBoost

📏

1.4 Scaling

Distances, penalties and gradient descent need it; trees don't. Fit the scaler on training data only.

Wine kNN: 69% raw → 95% scaled; the tree scores 89% either way. Unscaled features stretch the loss bowl: 100+ gradient steps instead of 1.

🧰 StandardScaler, MinMaxScaler, RobustScaler, QuantileTransformer

🛠️

1.5 Feature engineering

Put the shape of the answer in a column: ratios, date parts, sin/cos for cycles, interactions, point-in-time aggregates, text counts.

A line can't divide: distance ÷ speed must be added. Same linear model, validation R² 0.57 → 0.86. Aggregates use earlier rows only, never the current label.

🧰 pandas .dt / .str / groupby().transform(shift), PolynomialFeatures, SplineTransformer, Featuretools

🔗

1.6 Pipelines

Every data-dependent step goes inside the Pipeline; pass raw X to cross_val_score. Check get_feature_names_out().

Selecting features on all rows: 0.76 (docs) or 75–85% (Playground) on pure noise; inside the pipeline, 0.5. One fitted object is what you deploy.

🧰 make_pipeline, make_column_transformer, make_column_selector, set_output, joblib

🗣️ Teach it back

Answer out loud, like in an interview: “You're given a customer table with numbers, text categories, dates, blanks and some absurd values, and asked for a churn model by Friday. Walk me through everything you do to the data before and around the model, and how you make sure the validation score you report is real.”

A strong answer covers: EDA on the training split (types, missing rates, ranges, the target's balance and the majority baseline, group-bys against the target, a hunt for leaked columns with “would I know this at prediction time?”); missing values by mechanism with median fill plus indicators or a NaN-native model; outliers as errors vs real rare cases, the IQR rule, robust scaling; encoders chosen by cardinality, with target encoding cross-fitted; scaling for distance and gradient-based models only; a handful of domain features (ratios, date parts, point-in-time aggregates); and all of it in one ColumnTransformer Pipeline evaluated with cross-validation (time-based if the data is ordered in time), then saved as one artifact.

🏗️ Build it yourself: will this invoice be paid late?

✏️ The problem

A B2B invoicing startup wants to warn its users which invoices will be paid more than 30 days late, at the moment an invoice is created. You have 120,000 past invoices from 8,000 client companies over 3 years; about 15% were paid late. Columns: invoice_id, client_id, amount (from $50 to $2 million, a few obvious typos), currency (12 values), client_industry (40 values, missing for 18% of clients), client_country (25), issue_date, due_date, payment_terms (net 15/30/60), free-text notes, paid_date, and reminder_count (reminders sent before payment).

Using this phase's ideas, design the data preparation and validation: which columns you use and how, what you build, what you drop, how you check it's honest. Then open the reveal.

Reveal: one good answer

Requirements

  • Predict at invoice creation, so every feature must be known then. Output a probability of “more than 30 days late” to rank invoices for a warning.
  • Baseline to beat: always “on time” is 85% accurate and useless, so judge by AUC and precision among the top-ranked invoices (Lessons 5.3–5.4).

EDA and leakage (1.1)

  • Drop paid_date (it is the label) and reminder_count (reminders happen after the due date, mostly for late payers: a classic leak).
  • invoice_id: check it doesn't encode time or client in a leaky way, then drop it.
  • Plot lateness by month: if the late rate drifts over 3 years, validation must be by time.

Missing values and outliers (1.2)

  • client_industry missing for 18%: a constant “missing” category (for one-hot) plus the knowledge that small, informal clients often skip it, which may itself predict lateness.
  • amount: convert to one currency first; flag impossible values (negative, or 1,000× the client's usual); use log(amount) so $2 million doesn't dominate.

Encoding (1.3)

  • currency, country, payment_terms: one-hot (12 + 25 + 3 columns).
  • client_industry (40): one-hot with min_frequency, or target encoding.
  • client_id (8,000): never one-hot as dense (120,000 × 8,000 × 8 bytes ≈ 7.7 GB). Use history features instead (below), or cross-fitted target encoding computed only from earlier invoices.

Features (1.5)

  • Point-in-time client history, from invoices paid before this invoice's issue date: past late rate (smoothed toward the global 15%), number of past invoices, average days late, days since the last late payment. New clients get the global rate plus a “no history” flag.
  • Ratios and differences: days between issue and due date; amount ÷ the client's median past amount (an unusually large invoice is riskier).
  • Dates: issue month, day of week, is_quarter_end. Text: notes word count, mentions of “PO” or “installment”.

Model, scaling and pipeline (1.4, 1.6)

  • Baseline: logistic regression in a Pipeline with a ColumnTransformer (impute, one-hot, StandardScaler on numerics). Main model: HistGradientBoosting (no scaling needed, NaN native, Lesson 4.4).
  • History features are computed with timestamps before the split; every fitted step (imputers, encoders, scalers) lives in the Pipeline.

Validation plan

  • Train on years 1–2, validate on the first half of year 3, test on the second half: a time split, never a random one (Lesson 7.1). Optionally group by client to check performance on new clients (Lesson 5.1).
  • Report AUC and precision in the top 10% of risk, compared with the “client's past late rate” rule as a non-ML baseline.

Classic mistakes

  • Using reminder_count or anything computed from paid_date: a model with 0.98 AUC that can't be used.
  • Client history computed over all 3 years, including invoices after the one being predicted.
  • Random train/test split: the same client's simultaneous invoices land on both sides and flatter the score.
  • Mixing currencies: a ¥1,000,000 invoice treated as a $1,000,000 one.

🏆 Boss quiz

1. A category has 4 rows with targets 1, 0, 1, 1. The global mean is 0.25 and the smoothing is m = 4. What's its smoothed target encoding?
(n·mean + m·global) ÷ (n + m) = (4 × 0.75 + 4 × 0.25) ÷ 8 = (3 + 1) ÷ 8 = 0.5: halfway, because the 4 rows count as much as the 4 “virtual” rows of the global mean (Lesson 1.3).
2. Your RBF SVM's accuracy jumps from 61% to 88% after adding StandardScaler; your random forest's doesn't move. Why the difference?
Distance-, penalty- and gradient-based models are unit-sensitive; tree splits are invariant to monotone transforms of a feature (Lesson 1.4).
3. 40% of rows are missing blood_pressure, mostly for patients who were never admitted. You'll use logistic regression. What's the best first move?
The blank says “not admitted”, real information (the 2018 BMJ study: whether a test is ordered predicts survival). Dropping 40% of rows biases the sample; 0 is an impossible blood pressure the model would take literally (Lesson 1.2).
4. Which of these features is leakage-safe for predicting whether today's order will be returned?
Only the past, and never the current label. The all-orders rate includes today's (and future) returns; the refund is the outcome itself (Lessons 1.1 and 1.5).
5. A grid search tunes 5 values of C × 2 imputer strategies with cv=4 on a Pipeline. How many times is the imputer fitted in total, counting the final refit?
5 × 2 = 10 settings × 4 folds = 40 pipeline fits, plus 1 refit on all the training data = 41, each with its own imputer fitted only on that fit's training rows (Lesson 1.6).
