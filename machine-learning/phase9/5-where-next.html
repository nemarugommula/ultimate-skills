Where to go next: deep learning, books, courses and Kaggle

You can now frame, build, validate, explain and ship classical ML. This lesson maps what comes after: when deep learning takes over, which sibling course or book fits your goal, and a planner that turns “I should keep learning” into a schedule.

🎯 Hook: the methods you just learned are still winning

In 2022, Grinsztajn, Oyallon and Varoquaux benchmarked deep-learning models against tree ensembles like XGBoost and random forests on 45 tabular datasets, with about 20,000 compute hours of hyperparameter search per learner. Their result: on medium-sized data (around 10,000 rows), tree-based models remained state of the art, even before counting how much faster they are.

So the toolkit from this course isn't a warm-up for “real” ML: it's what most companies' tables still need. The interesting question is where its edge ends, and what to learn for the problems beyond it.

🔮 Predict first

Which of these problems is the clearest case for deep learning rather than the methods in this course?

The photos. Raw pixels have no ready-made columns; a convolutional network learns the features (edges, textures, shapes) itself. Churn and demand are tables with meaningful columns, where boosted trees are the strong default. Sort eight more problems in Playground 2.

🧠 Analogy: after the language course, the trip

You've finished a good language course: grammar, vocabulary, a few conversations. Another grammar book will help a little. A month in the country, ordering food, getting lost and asking directions, will help a lot, and it will show you exactly which grammar you still need.

Your “trip” is a project on data you care about. The books and courses below are the phrasebooks you'll reach for while you're there.

🤔 Why this? Why pick a next step by goal (and a project) instead of “learn everything”?
  1. The problem before. After a first course, the field looks infinite: deep learning, LLMs, RL, MLOps, causal inference, Bayesian methods. People collect certificates in all of them and finish none, or jump straight to transformers without ever shipping a model.
  2. What people tried. Following someone else's roadmap end to end. Roadmaps are useful maps but poor itineraries: they don't know whether you're aiming at analytics, ML engineering or research, or how many hours a week you have.
  3. Why this works. A goal cuts the map down to 3–4 steps, and hours per week turn them into a date. Tiny example: at 5 hours a week, a plan of a 25-hour project, 15 hours of reading and a 20-hour Kaggle competition is 60 hours = 12 weeks, a quarter. At 10 hours a week it's 6 weeks. That's a plan you can say yes or no to (Playground 1).
  4. What it costs, when it fails. Narrowing means skipping things that might matter later, and hour estimates are guesses (ours are rough, yours will differ). Revisit the plan every month; if a project shows you a gap (say, you can't explain a model's errors), go back to that lesson first.
  5. What came next. The graduation page turns this into a skills map across the whole course and a portfolio plan; the sibling courses continue each branch: the Deep Learning course (being written), Reinforcement Learning, AI Engineer and Math for AI.

Where deep learning takes over

Deep learning means neural networks with many layers that learn their own features from raw inputs. Classical ML needs you (or a pipeline) to supply meaningful columns; deep nets earn their cost when no good columns exist, or when there's so much data that learned features beat hand-made ones.

DataDefaultWhy
Tables with meaningful columns (customers, payments, sensors summaries)Gradient boostingFeatures already exist; trees handle mixed types, missing values and irregular patterns (Lesson 4.6)
Images, videoConvolutional nets, vision transformers, or a pretrained model fine-tunedFeatures (edges, textures, objects) must be learned from pixels
Audio, speechPretrained speech modelsSame: raw waveforms, learned features
TextTF-IDF + linear model for small labelled sets; pretrained transformers or LLM APIs for meaningClassical NLP (Lesson 7.5) is cheap and strong; transformers capture context and meaning
Very large tables with high-cardinality IDsBoosting, then neural nets with embeddings at large scaleEmbeddings for millions of IDs; e.g. Stripe moved Radar from XGBoost + a neural net to a neural net only (Lesson 9.2)

🔮 Predict first

A team has 50 labelled examples of support emails for each of 5 teams and wants emails routed automatically next week. What's the most sensible first tool?

With only 250 labels and a deadline, a pretrained language model used through an API (few-shot) is fastest and usually strong. Training a deep net from scratch needs far more data. Once you've collected a few thousand labelled emails, a TF-IDF + logistic regression classifier (Lesson 7.5) is a cheap, fast, auditable alternative worth comparing against.

🧰 Tools you'll actually use

Whatever you do next, finish projects the way this course taught: a baseline, honest cross-validation, a saved artifact and a note a stranger can trust. This is the minimal skeleton (scikit-learn 1.9; runs on 1.6 too):

import json, platform
import joblib, sklearn
from sklearn.datasets import load_breast_cancer
from sklearn.dummy import DummyClassifier
from sklearn.ensemble import HistGradientBoostingClassifier
from sklearn.model_selection import cross_val_score

X, y = load_breast_cancer(return_X_y=True, as_frame=True)
results = {}
for name, model in [("baseline", DummyClassifier()), ("hgb", HistGradientBoostingClassifier(random_state=0))]:
    s = cross_val_score(model, X, y, cv=5, scoring="roc_auc")
    results[name] = f"{s.mean():.3f} ± {s.std():.3f}"
print(results)

final = HistGradientBoostingClassifier(random_state=0).fit(X, y)
joblib.dump(final, "model.joblib")                       # the artifact
json.dump({"cv_roc_auc": results, "sklearn": sklearn.__version__,  # what a reader needs to trust it
           "python": platform.python_version(), "features": list(X.columns)},
          open("model_card.json", "w"), indent=2)

Prints {'baseline': '0.500 ± 0.000', 'hgb': '0.993 ± 0.004'}. Lab 9 grows this into a full project with a leakage-safe split, a cost-based threshold, a calibration check and a complete model card. For deep learning, the next tool is PyTorch (Géron's new edition and fast.ai both use it).

🚀 Startup vs big tech

Startup path

Breadth pays: classical ML plus enough deep learning and LLM APIs to pick the right tool, plus deployment and monitoring. Learn MLOps by shipping (Made With ML, DMLS), and keep the System Design course close: you'll own the whole stack.

Big-tech path

Depth pays: become the person for one area (ranking, forecasting, experimentation, fraud) and know its literature. Read ESL and the papers behind your systems (the Facebook and Google ad papers in Lesson 9.2 are a good start) and learn the platform tools your company runs.

🎤 Interview angle

“What are you learning right now, and why?” A strong answer:

  • Names one topic tied to the role (“calibration and uplift, because your team runs retention offers”).
  • Mentions a concrete artifact: a project, a notebook, a write-up with numbers.
  • Shows judgement about tools: when you'd use deep learning or an LLM and when a boosted tree is the better first move.
🏭 In the real world
Trees vs deep learning on tables 🧪 Research · 2022 Why do tree-based models still outperform deep learning on tabular data?

How: 45 tabular datasets, ~20,000 compute hours of tuning per learner, trees vs deep tabular models.

Why this method: evidence that the classical toolkit is still the right default for medium-sized tables, and a list of what tabular neural nets must learn to do (ignore uninformative features, learn irregular functions).

When deep learning took over a tabular system 🏢 Stripe · 2023 How we built it: Stripe Radar

How: moved fraud scoring from an XGBoost + neural-net ensemble to a neural net only in mid-2022, then scaled up the training data.

Why this method: at their data size the neural net kept improving with more data, while XGBoost was slower to retrain and didn't benefit as much. Scale changes the answer.

The free textbook path 📜 Books · 2009–2023 ISL with R and Python · ESL

How: the authors provide free PDFs; ISLP has a Python lab at the end of each chapter.

Why this method: the most-cited theory references for exactly the methods in this course, at two levels of math.

Practice in public 🧰 Community Kaggle competitions

How: real datasets with hidden test sets, discussion forums and winners' write-ups.

Why this method: the fastest way to practise validation discipline and to read how strong practitioners build features and ensembles (Lesson 9.3).

🤯 Fun fact · a 2009 textbook that still wins

The free PDF of The Elements of Statistical Learning on the authors' site is the corrected 12th printing (January 2017) of the 2nd edition from February 2009. Its chapters on boosting, random forests and regularization describe the same families that a 2022 benchmark of 45 datasets still found to be state of the art on medium-sized tables.

Check yourself

1. Your plan has a 30-hour project, 20 hours of reading and a 10-hour Kaggle entry. At 4 hours a week, how long will it take?
30 + 20 + 10 = 60 hours ÷ 4 per week = 15 weeks, a bit over three months. If that's too long, cut scope (one project, fewer chapters) rather than hoping for more hours.
2. A manager asks whether the team should replace its gradient-boosting credit-risk model (60 columns, 300,000 rows) with deep learning “because it's more modern”. What do you say?
Benchmarks (Grinsztajn et al., 2022; Lesson 4.6) show trees remain state of the art on medium tabular data. Neural nets can win at very large scale or with unstructured inputs (Stripe Radar, Lesson 9.2), but that's a hypothesis to test, not a reason to rewrite.
3. You want to become an ML engineer and have three months. Which plan fits best?
ML engineering is judged on shipping: pipelines, serving, monitoring (Lessons 8.5–8.6, 9.1). A deployed project plus the two MLOps resources builds exactly that and gives you a story for interviews. ESL is great theory, but three months of it won't show you can ship.
