Recap: Trees & ensembles

Decision trees, random forests, boosting, the three big GBM libraries, stacking, and why all of it still rules tabular data. Here's the phase on one page, a tuning game with a budget, a project to design yourself, and Lab 4.

🧾 Cheat sheet

🌳

4.1 Decision trees

Gini = 1 − Σp² (0.5 for 50/50); entropy = −Σp log₂p (1 bit). Gain = parent − weighted children. Tame with max_depth, min_samples_leaf, ccp_alpha.

Greedy yes/no questions on one feature at a time. No scaling needed; staircase boundaries; flat outside the training range; deep trees memorize noise.

🧰 DecisionTreeClassifier, export_text, cost_complexity_pruning_path

🌲

4.2 Bagging & random forests

Var(average) = ρσ² + (1 − ρ)σ²/B. ~37% of rows are out-of-bag per tree. Classifier default max_features="sqrt".

Average many deep trees trained on bootstrap samples and random feature subsets: variance falls, bias stays low. More trees never overfit; OOB gives a free estimate.

🧰 RandomForestClassifier(oob_score=True), ExtraTrees, BaggingClassifier

🚀

4.3 Boosting

AdaBoost: α = ½ ln((1 − err)/err). Gradient boosting: F ← F + η · tree(residuals). η 0.03–0.1 + early stopping.

Small trees in sequence, each fixing what's still wrong: bias falls. Boosting can overfit by adding rounds; halving η roughly doubles the rounds needed.

🧰 HistGradientBoosting*, GradientBoosting*(n_iter_no_change), AdaBoostClassifier

⚙️

4.4 XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost

Leaf value w* = −G/(H + λ); split gain minus γ. ≤ 255 histogram bins. num_leaves = 31. Ordered target statistics.

XGBoost: regularized Newton steps, missing-value directions. LightGBM: histograms, leaf-wise growth, GOSS, EFB. CatBoost: no row sees its own label; symmetric trees.

🧰 xgboost 3.4, lightgbm 4.7, catboost 1.2, scikit-learn HGB

🧩

4.5 Stacking & blending

Blend variance = w²σA² + (1 − w)²σB² + 2w(1 − w)ρσAσB. Meta-model on out-of-fold predictions only.

Diverse models' errors cancel; near-copies don't help. In-sample stacking overtrusts overfit models. Gains are small; latency and maintenance are not (Netflix shipped 2 models).

🧰 StackingClassifier(cv=5), VotingClassifier, cross_val_predict, AutoGluon

📊

4.6 Trees vs deep learning on tables

45 datasets, ~10k rows: tuned GBDTs win. Three reasons: irregular targets, useless features, meaningful column orientation. TabPFN: ≤ 10k rows, 2.8 s vs 4 h tuning.

Deep learning wins on images, text, audio, huge and multimodal data. Foundation models are strong on small tables; check TabArena, inference cost and licences.

🧰 GBDT first; tabpfn on small data; TabArena for the current standings

🗣️ Teach it back

Answer out loud, like in an interview: “You have a 200,000-row table for predicting customer churn. Walk me from a single decision tree to the model you'd actually ship. At each step, what problem does the next idea fix, and what does it cost? Then: your colleague wants a 10-model stack for +0.2% AUC. What do you say?”

A strong answer covers: a tree's greedy impurity splits and why a deep one overfits (variance); random forests averaging decorrelated deep trees (ρσ² + (1 − ρ)σ²/B, OOB); boosting adding shallow trees on residuals to cut bias, with a learning rate and early stopping; picking a library (CatBoost for many high-cardinality categoricals, LightGBM for huge or sparse data, HGB with no extra dependency); why trees beat neural nets on this kind of table; and for the stack: diverse models, out-of-fold training, then weighing 0.2% against latency, retraining and monitoring for 10 models, as Netflix did in 2012.

🏗️ Build it yourself: the churn model for a mobile operator

✏️ The problem

A mobile operator has 400,000 customers and wants, every night, the probability that each one leaves in the next 30 days, so a retention team of 20 people can call the riskiest. About 4% churn per month. There are 12 months of history and 60 columns: 45 numeric (usage, bills, dropped calls; about 12% missing for new customers), 15 categorical, including postcode (9,000 levels) and device_model (1,200 levels). The team also wants the top reasons for each flagged customer. Nightly scoring must finish within an hour on one CPU server.

Using this phase, sketch the model, the validation plan, the metrics and the traps. Then open the reveal.

Reveal: one good answer

Requirements

  • A calibrated probability per customer (it will be ranked and maybe used for offer budgets), refreshed nightly, retrained monthly.
  • Per-customer explanations; no future information in the features; a clear win over a simple baseline.

Numbers

  • Churners per month: 4% × 400,000 = 16,000. The team can call maybe 20 × 60 = 1,200 a day, so precision in the top few percent matters more than overall accuracy.
  • Training rows: one row per customer per month (a snapshot and the next 30 days' label): about 400,000 × 10 ≈ 4 million rows for months 1–10.
  • Scoring: even at the 0.0002 s per row that the TabPFN paper measured for default CatBoost, 400,000 × 0.0002 s = 80 s. Comfortably within the hour; batch prediction is usually faster.

Model choice, and why

  • Baseline: logistic regression on a few scaled features, and a default HistGradientBoostingClassifier. Anything fancier must beat both.
  • Main model: CatBoost with postcode and device_model as cat_features: ordered target statistics handle 9,000 levels without the target leak of naive target encoding (4.4); strong defaults; symmetric trees score fast. LightGBM with cross-fitted target encoding is a fine alternative at 4 million rows.
  • Missing usage values: leave them as NaN; GBMs learn a direction for missing values. Add a “months since joining” feature so the model can tell new customers apart.
  • Not a neural network: a plain medium-sized table with thresholds and many weak columns is GBDT territory (4.6). Not a 10-model stack: the gain rarely survives the maintenance cost (4.5).

Validation plan

  • Split by time, not at random: train on months 1–9, early-stop on month 10, choose settings on month 11, report once on month 12 (Lessons 0.6, 7.1). A random split leaks the future.
  • Early stopping on the validation month with η ≈ 0.05; cap depth (6) or leaves (31); tune a few knobs with Optuna (5.7).

Metrics

  • Precision in the top 1,200 per day and PR-AUC (Lesson 5.4): with 4% positives, accuracy is useless (96% for “nobody churns”).
  • Log-loss and a calibration plot (Lesson 5.6), because the probabilities drive budgets. SHAP values per customer for the reasons (Lesson 8.2).

Classic mistakes

  • Features that leak the answer: contract_end_reason, or usage measured after the snapshot date.
  • Target-encoding postcode on the full training set (each rare postcode encodes its own label).
  • num_leaves=255 on small segments: deep, lopsided trees that memorize.
  • Expecting the model to extrapolate to a brand-new price plan it has never seen: trees predict within what they've seen.
  • Reporting the score on the month used for early stopping (it's optimistic). Keep one untouched test month.

What this design doesn't cover yet: picking the threshold and handling the 4% class imbalance (5.5), checking calibration (5.6), and monitoring drift after deployment (8.6).

🏆 Boss quiz

1. A node holds 9 rows of class A and 3 of class B. A split sends 6 A's left (pure) and 3 A's + 3 B's right. What's the Gini gain?
Parent: 1 − 0.75² − 0.25² = 0.375. Left (6 A's): 0. Right (3/3): 0.5. Weighted children: 6/12 × 0 + 6/12 × 0.5 = 0.25. Gain = 0.375 − 0.25 = 0.125 (Lesson 4.1). 0.375 is the parent's impurity, not the gain; 0.25 is the children's.
2. Your random forest's trees correlate at ρ = 0.4. Going from 100 to 1,000 trees reduces the variance (as a share of one tree's) from about…
ρ + (1 − ρ)/B: 0.4 + 0.6/100 = 0.406; 0.4 + 0.6/1000 = 0.4006. The floor ρ = 0.4 dominates (Lesson 4.2).
3. A GBM with learning rate 0.1 is best at round 120 on validation. You switch to learning rate 0.05. Where do you expect the new best round, and what should you do?
Halving η roughly doubles the rounds needed (η × rounds stays about constant, Lesson 4.3). Set a generous maximum and use early stopping on a validation set.
4. Which pairing of library and fix is correct?
Ordered target statistics are CatBoost's; GOSS (and EFB, histograms, leaf-wise growth) are LightGBM's; XGBoost introduced the regularized second-order objective and sparsity-aware splits (Lesson 4.4).
5. You have 6,000 rows and 30 numeric columns, accuracy matters most, and predictions run once a week. A teammate built a deep MLP that loses to your GBDT. Best next experiment?
Small table, accuracy first, latency irrelevant: exactly where TabPFN beat tuned GBDTs in the Nature paper; an out-of-fold blend can add a little more (Lessons 4.5–4.6). Rotating features hurts trees; a bigger MLP doesn't address why MLPs struggle on tables.
