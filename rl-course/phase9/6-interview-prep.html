Phase 9 · Lesson 6

Interview prep: RL research & engineering

The questions research teams actually ask, mapped to what you've learned in this course.

🎯 Hook

RL interviews usually mix four things: concepts (explain the Bellman equation, why PPO clips), derivations (the policy gradient), coding (implement GAE or a GRPO loss from scratch), and research discussion (walk through a project, critique a paper). This course covered all four, and this page is for practice.

💡 Big idea Interviewers care less about memorized formulas than about whether you can reason from first principles: explain trade-offs, derive the key step, spot bugs, and say "here's how I'd test that". Practice explaining out loud.
🧠 Analogy: a system-design interview

Nobody passes a system-design interview by reciting one company's architecture. You talk through requirements, propose a design, name the trade-offs, and say how you'd test it. RL interviews work the same way: "why does PPO clip?" is answered by the problem it solves and what breaks without it, not by the formula alone.

🎮 Playground · Flashcards

Coding exercises to practice (no notes, 30–45 min each)

  1. Tabular Q-learning on a gridworld you write yourself (Lab 3).
  2. Compute discounted returns and GAE for a batch with done flags (5.5), vectorized.
  3. The PPO clipped loss with value and entropy terms (5.6).
  4. The DPO loss from per-sequence log-probs (7.5).
  5. GRPO: group advantages + token-level clipped loss with a response mask (Lab 8).
  6. The unbiased pass@k estimator (8.8) and a bootstrap CI (9.3).

Grade yourself with the labs: copy the lab function's signature into a fresh file (e.g. Lab 5's gae or ppo_policy_loss for exercises 2–3), delete the body, write it from memory, then paste it back and re-run that lab's check()s (they sit right after each TODO, so you can stop before training starts). Green checks = interview-ready. Exercise 6 maps to Lab 9's pass_at_k and bootstrap_ci the same way.

Tip The "walk me through your project" question

Prepare a 3-minute story for your best project: question → why it matters → what you built → main result with error bars → something that surprised you or went wrong → what you'd do next. Expect follow-ups: "how do you know it isn't noise?", "what's the strongest baseline?", "what would change at 100× scale?"

🌍 Where it's used
The unbiased pass@k estimator 🧪 Inside · 2021 Codex and HumanEval (Chen et al., 2021)

How: introduced with HumanEval: sample n programs per problem, count the c that pass the unit tests, and estimate the chance that at least one of k samples would pass.

Why this method: drawing exactly k samples per estimate is noisy and wasteful. One batch of n gives a steadier estimate for every k at once, which is why exercise 6 asks for it.

GRPO loss with a response mask 🧪 Inside · 2025 verl, an open LLM-RL library

How: the group advantages and clipped token-level loss of exercise 5 are built into verl, which also runs PPO and DAPO. DAPO's own training ran on it.

Why this method: production code is this loss plus engineering. Interviewers ask you to write it because knowing every line lets you debug a library instead of just calling it.

The DPO loss from sequence log-probs 🧪 Inside · 2023 Hugging Face TRL: DPOTrainer

How: sums response log-probs for the policy and a frozen reference, applies the DPO loss (exercise 4), and logs chosen/rejected log-probs and implicit-reward margins.

Why this method: those logged numbers only help if you know the formula. Falling chosen log-probs can be likelihood displacement (7.5), not a bug.

🤯 Fun fact

A common interview move is to ask for something you've only used as a library call, like "derive why ∇log π gives an unbiased gradient" or "why does PPO clip the ratio instead of the loss?" If you worked through Phases 5 and 8, you've already derived these.

🌉 Roles to look at

Research engineer (RL infrastructure, training pipelines: your SWE background is a big advantage), research scientist (often needs publications or a strong research portfolio), and applied RL / post-training engineer (building RL environments, reward models and evals). Many people move from research engineering to research.

Check yourself ✅

1. "Why does PPO clip the probability ratio?" What's the best way to open your answer?
Problem → mechanism → trade-off shows understanding. Then write the formula, and mention what you'd log to check it (clip fraction, KL).
2. Mid-way through coding GAE you're unsure how to handle done flags. Best move?
Interviewers want to see how you reason and test. Done flags are not an edge case: getting them wrong is one of the most common RL bugs (Lab 4).
3. "How do you know your result isn't noise?" Best answer?
Numbers, protocol and the right test (9.3). Curves can look clearly different from seed noise alone (Henderson et al.).
📝 TL;DR
