Phase 9 · Lesson 5

Portfolio projects that get you hired

Courses show you studied. Projects show you can do research. Here's what to build, sized to your compute.

🧰 You'll need (quick reminders)
  • Seeds, CIs and ablations: several runs, an interval for the true average, and removing one piece at a time to show what causes the gain (9.3).
  • The write-up template: question → what you did → what you found, with error bars → limitations (9.4).
🎯 Hook

Within weeks of DeepSeek-R1 (January 2025), a small team led by Berkeley PhD student Jiayi Pan released TinyZero: a reproduction of the "R1-Zero" effect (a model learning to check its own reasoning through RL alone) on Countdown number puzzles with a 3B model, for under $30 of compute. It spread widely online and in the press. You don't need a big budget to get noticed: you need a small, clean, well-explained experiment.

💡 Big idea Research teams look for evidence that you can implement RL correctly, run rigorous experiments and communicate findings. So a strong project = a clear question + a correct implementation + multiple seeds and ablations + an honest write-up + runnable code. One deep project beats ten shallow ones. Your software background is a real advantage: clean, tested, reproducible code stands out in a field full of messy research scripts.
🧠 Analogy: a green CI badge

A portfolio is a repo with a green CI badge: a reviewer trusts it before reading a line. Seeds, confidence intervals, a one-command reproduction and a clear README are your badge. They tell a hiring manager the result is real before they dig into your code.

🎮 Playground · Pick a project

Size, part-time: ★ = a weekend · ★★ = 1–2 weeks · ★★★ = 2–4 weeks · ★★★★ = a month or more. 🏁 = the first milestone to hit before anything else.

What makes a project stand out

  • A specific question, e.g. "Does dropping std normalization change response length in GRPO on small models?", not "I implemented GRPO".
  • Baselines + ablations + seeds with confidence intervals (9.3).
  • Something surprising or useful: a bug in common practice, a negative result, a reproduction gap, a clean tool.
  • Presentation: README with the main figure, one-command reproduction, a blog post (9.4).
🌍 Where it's used
Engineering strengths: tests and benchmarks 🧪 Research · 2021 Stable-Baselines3 (Raffin et al., JMLR 2021)

How: standard RL algorithms behind one consistent interface, benchmarked against reference code bases, with automated unit tests covering 95% of the code and extensive docs.

Why this method: in RL a silent bug just looks like a bad seed. Tests and benchmarks are what let others trust the code, and they're a software engineer's home turf.

Fit the question to your compute 🧪 Research · 2021 Jones: Scaling Scaling Laws with Board Games

How: AlphaZero-style agents on the board game Hex at many board sizes and compute budgets. Strength at fixed compute fell predictably as the board grew, and training and thinking-time compute could be traded.

Why this method: a series of small, cheap runs can predict big ones, so a question about trends is answerable without a big lab's budget.

🤯 Fun fact

Karpathy's "Pong from Pixels" (2016), a classic introduction to policy gradients, is built around an agent that is, in his words, "130 lines of Python only using numpy". He trained it for 3 nights to get a policy "slightly better than the AI player". A small project, explained clearly, can outlast much bigger ones.

🌉 Suggested path

1) Polish two labs into public repos with write-ups (e.g. Lab 5 PPO + an ablation, Lab 8 GRPO + a mini-research idea). 2) Do one reproduction (9.2). 3) Do the capstone (9.7). That's a portfolio that holds up in research interviews.

Check yourself ✅

1. Which portfolio entry would impress a research team most?
A specific question answered rigorously and reproducibly shows research skill. A list of implementations shows you followed tutorials; a screenshot shows no evidence at all.
2. You only have a laptop CPU. What's a good portfolio project?
Classic-RL projects run in minutes on a CPU and show the same rigor. A 7B model on a CPU would mostly show patience.
3. Your project found no effect: the 95% CI of the difference straddles 0. Is it still portfolio-worthy?
A careful negative result is useful and shows judgment. Trying settings until something "works", then reporting only that one, is cherry-picking (9.3).
📝 TL;DR
  • Depth over breadth: question, correct implementation, seeds and ablations, write-up, code.
  • Fit the project to your compute: laptop and Colab are enough for meaningful work.
  • Use your engineering strengths: tests, clean code, reproducibility.
