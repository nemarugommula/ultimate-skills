The post-training pipeline: pretrain → SFT → RL

How real labs turn a raw text predictor into a helpful assistant, and later a reasoning model. Four real recipes, side by side.

🧰 You'll need (quick reminders)
  • Behavior cloning = copying expert examples, and why copying alone has limits (Lesson 6.7).
  • Why RL after copying: practice + scores can go beyond the examples (Lesson 0.4).
🎯 Hook

For InstructGPT (2022), OpenAI's whole RLHF dataset was surprisingly small: about 13,000 prompts with human demonstrations for SFT, 33,000 prompts with human rankings for the reward model, and 31,000 prompts for PPO, written and labeled by a team of roughly 40 contractors. That was enough to make a 1.3B model preferred over the 175B GPT-3. On 30 November 2022, OpenAI launched ChatGPT, which its announcement called a "sibling model to InstructGPT", trained with the same recipe.

💡 Big idea Pretraining builds knowledge (predict the internet). SFT teaches format and behavior (imitate good examples). Preference / RL stages optimize what we actually want (helpful, harmless, correct), going beyond the examples. Modern recipes repeat and mix these stages.
🧠 Analogy: training a doctor

Medical school textbooks give broad knowledge (pretraining). Residency means shadowing senior doctors and copying how they handle patients (SFT). Then the young doctor practises on their own and gets feedback on outcomes (RL). Each stage builds on the one before; skipping one shows.

Math refresher Why SFT before RL?

A concrete failure first. Ask a base model "What is the capital of France?" and it may continue with "What is the capital of Germany? What is the capital of…", because on the internet a question is often followed by more questions (quiz pages, forum lists). It's predicting the web, not answering you. SFT on (question → answer) conversations teaches it that a user's question is followed by an assistant's answer.

RL improves what the policy already sometimes does. If the model never produces a well-formatted answer, every sample gets reward 0 and the gradient is zero (the exploration problem, Lesson 6.5). SFT puts probability mass on reasonable behaviors first, so RL has something to reinforce. DeepSeek-R1-Zero showed that a strong enough base model can skip SFT, but its outputs were messy (mixed languages, poor readability). That's why the final R1 added a small "cold-start" SFT stage.

✏️ Worked example: why RL needs SFT's head start

RL samples 8 answers per prompt and learns only if at least one earns a reward. The chance of that is 1 − (1 − p)8, where p = the chance a single answer is good:

  • Base model, p = 0.001 (1 good answer in 1,000): 1 − 0.9998 ≈ 0.8%. On 99 of 100 prompts, every sample scores 0 and there's nothing to learn from.
  • After SFT, p = 0.4: 1 − 0.68 ≈ 98%. Almost every prompt gives RL a good answer to reinforce.

🌍 Where it's used
SFT → reward model → PPO 🏭 In production · 2022 OpenAI: InstructGPT became the API default

How: SFT on labeler-written demos gives the starting policy. Labelers rank 4–9 answers per prompt to train the reward model, then PPO optimizes the policy against it.

Why this method: ranking answers is cheaper and more consistent than writing perfect ones, and SFT first means RL starts from a model that already follows instructions.

Rounds of rejection sampling + SFT + DPO 🏭 In production · 2024 Meta: The Llama 3 Herd of Models

How: each of six rounds: sample 10–30 answers per prompt, keep the one the reward model scores highest, SFT on those, then DPO on fresh preference pairs. The new policy makes the next round's data.

Why this method: Meta tried PPO, but found DPO needed less compute at this scale and did better. Repeating rounds with fresh samples recovers some of on-policy RL's freshness.

SFT → DPO → RL on checkable answers 🧪 Research · 2024 Ai2: Tülu 3, a fully open recipe

How: SFT on curated prompts, DPO on preference pairs that include its own answers, then a final RL stage where the reward is a fixed bonus only when a maths answer or instruction constraint is verified.

Why this method: each stage uses the cheapest signal that works: demos for format, preferences for style, a checker where one exists. Data, code and weights are all public, so you can study every stage.

🤯 Fun fact

DeepSeek-R1's report describes using R1 to generate about 800,000 training examples, which were then used to fine-tune much smaller open models (Qwen and Llama, from 1.5B to 70B) with plain SFT. These distilled models gained strong reasoning skills without any RL of their own, and on several benchmarks they beat running RL directly on the small models. Distillation from an RL-trained teacher is now a standard step.

Check yourself ✅

1. Which stage is behavior cloning?
SFT imitates expert answers token by token, with the same strengths and exposure-bias weaknesses as BC.
2. Tülu 3 popularized which stage name?
Tülu 3's final stage trained against programmatic checkers (math answers, instruction constraints) instead of a learned reward model.
3. Why did DeepSeek add a cold-start SFT stage before R1's RL?
R1-Zero proved RL alone can create reasoning. The cold start made it usable.
📝 TL;DR
  • Pretrain (knowledge) → SFT (format, imitation) → preference/RL (what we want) → often repeated.
  • InstructGPT: SFT → reward model → PPO. Llama 3: SFT + rejection sampling + DPO, in rounds. Tülu 3: SFT → DPO → RLVR. R1: cold start → GRPO → rejection-sampled SFT → RL.
  • SFT first so RL has good behaviors to reinforce. Distillation spreads RL gains to small models.
