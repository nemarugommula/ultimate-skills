Phase 8A · Lesson 7

Entropy collapse & exploration in LLMs

RL makes the model more confident, and an overconfident model stops exploring. The explore–exploit dilemma from Phase 1, at billion-parameter scale.

🧰 You'll need (quick reminders)
  • Explore vs exploit: an agent that stops exploring settles for the best option it has seen (Lesson 1.3).
  • Entropy = how spread out a policy's choices are (Lesson 6.2; reminder below).
🎯 Hook

Ask a fresh model the same question 6 times and you get 6 different approaches. After a lot of RL training, you often get the same answer, word for word, 6 times. A 2025 study (Cui et al., "The Entropy Mechanism of RL for Reasoning Language Models") found that across many runs, performance gains were "bought" by spending this variety, like a budget. Once the budget was spent, improvement stalled.

💡 Big idea Policy gradients push probability toward what worked, so the policy sharpens. Sharpening raises pass@1 but shrinks the variety of approaches. When every sample looks the same, groups get identical rewards (zero advantage), alternatives disappear, and the model can no longer discover anything new.
🧠 First: what's entropy again?

Entropy = how spread out the model's choices are. A die has high entropy (any face could come up). A loaded die that always shows 6 has zero entropy. For an LLM: high entropy = it tries different words and strategies; low entropy = it always says the same thing.

Analogy: a restaurant that keeps only its best-selling dish. Sales per dish go up (pass@1 ↑), but the regular who needed the vegetarian option stops coming (pass@k ↓), and you'll never discover that your new dish would have been a hit.

🎮 Playground · Watch a policy collapse

A model chooses among 6 solution strategies. Strategy A solves 80% of the 15 easy problems. Only strategy F can solve the 5 hard problems (40% of the time). RL updates the policy toward higher expected reward. The entropy bonus λ rewards staying spread out.

With no bonus, entropy collapses (1.79 → ~0.02): the policy goes all-in on strategy A. pass@1 doubles (0.29 → 0.60), but pass@16 falls (0.92 → 0.75), because the hard problems only strategy F could solve are lost. A large entropy bonus keeps pass@16 high, but pass@1 suffers, since it also keeps the useless strategies B–E alive. A blanket entropy bonus is a blunt instrument.

What researchers do about it

⬆️

Clip-higher (DAPO)

Give low-probability tokens more room to grow (8.4), so good rare alternatives survive.

🍴

Focus on "forking" tokens

A 2025 study found only a small minority of tokens (~20%) are high-entropy decision points ("wait", "alternatively", "so"), and training mainly on those worked as well or better. Protect exploration where choices actually branch.

🎯

Targeted regularizers

Methods like Clip-Cov / KL-Cov (Cui et al.) restrain only the tokens whose updates shrink entropy most, rather than adding entropy everywhere.

📚

Data & sampling

Harder prompts, curricula, dropping all-solved prompts, and objectives that value pass@k (like MaxRL, 8.4).

The cheapest knob: temperature

Numbers first: a model's next-token probabilities are 0.70 / 0.20 / 0.10. Sample at temperature T = 2 and they become about 0.52 / 0.28 / 0.20; at T = 0.5 they become 0.91 / 0.07 / 0.02. (Temperature divides the model's raw scores by T before turning them into probabilities: T > 1 flattens, T < 1 sharpens, T → 0 is "always pick the top token", i.e. greedy.)

Raising the rollout temperature is the cheapest exploration knob: no change to the loss, just more varied samples, so groups disagree more often and advantages aren't all zero. Too high and the samples turn into nonsense. Note that the training temperature (used to generate rollouts, often around 1.0) is usually not the evaluation temperature (often lower, or greedy). A model can look collapsed at T = 1 and fine when greedy, or the reverse. Lab 8 reports both for exactly this reason.

Math refresher Why policy gradients reduce entropy

A positive-advantage update raises a token's score and lowers the others. When the model already favors a token and it pays off, it becomes even more favored: rich get richer.

Cui et al. made this precise: entropy drops when "already confident" and "turned out well" go together. Statisticians call "two things moving together" covariance. Confident-and-right tokens shrink entropy; a surprising (low-probability) token that turns out right raises it. Since the model is usually confident in its winning tokens, entropy mostly goes down.

Their empirical fit, in words: performance ≈ ceiling − (something that shrinks as entropy is used up). Written as R ≈ −a·eH + b, where H is entropy and a, b are constants fitted per model: as H → 0, R approaches its ceiling −a + b and stops improving.

🌍 Where it's used
An entropy thermostat 🧪 Research · 2025 Skywork-OR1 (open reasoning models)

How: the policy's loss gets an entropy bonus whose weight rises automatically whenever entropy drops below a target value, so entropy can't collapse below that floor.

Why this method: a fixed bonus weight was too sensitive to the weight and the data to set in advance. A target entropy is one number that keeps exploration alive.

Temperature as a schedule 🏭 In production · 2025 Kimi K2 (Moonshot AI)

How: rollouts for creative writing and complex reasoning are sampled at a high temperature early in RL, then the temperature decays as training goes on.

Why this method: early on, diverse samples find good strategies and avoid settling too soon. Late in training, the same randomness just makes answers unreliable.

Train only the forking tokens 🧪 Research · 2025 Wang et al. (Qwen team): “Beyond the 80/20 rule”

How: the policy-gradient update is applied only to the 20% of tokens with the highest entropy, the points where the reasoning could branch.

Why this method: those tokens decide direction. It matched full updates on Qwen3-8B and beat them on Qwen3-32B (+11 points on AIME'25). Training only on the other 80% hurt.

🤯 Fun fact

This is the same story as ε-greedy vs greedy from Lesson 1.3: an agent that stops exploring settles on the best option it has seen, not the best that exists. Thirty years of RL theory about exploration came straight back into LLM research in 2025.

Check yourself ✅

1. What is entropy collapse in LLM RL?
Low entropy means near-identical samples, zero group advantages and no discovery.
2. In the playground with λ = 0, pass@1 rose but pass@16 fell. Why?
Sharpening helps the typical case and loses coverage of rare-but-necessary approaches.
3. Why is a uniform entropy bonus a blunt tool?
Preferred remedies (clip-higher, forking tokens, covariance-based clipping) focus where exploration pays off.
📝 TL;DR
  • RL sharpens the policy: entropy ↓, pass@1 ↑, diversity and pass@k often ↓.
  • Empirically, performance is bought with entropy (R ≈ −a·eH + b), and gains stall when it's spent.
  • Remedies: clip-higher, forking-token focus, covariance-based clipping, curricula, pass@k-aware objectives.
  • Temperature is the cheapest exploration knob. Training (rollout) temperature ≠ evaluation temperature: report which one you used.
