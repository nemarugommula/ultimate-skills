Process vs outcome rewards

Reward only the final answer, or every reasoning step? It's the credit-assignment problem from Lesson 0.1, inside a chain of thought.

🧰 You'll need (quick reminders)
  • Credit assignment: which of many steps deserved the credit or blame? (Lesson 0.1)
  • Value = expected outcome from a given point (Lesson 2.3), and it can be estimated by trying many times and averaging (Monte Carlo, Lesson 3.1).
🎯 Hook

In 2023, OpenAI paid human labelers to grade 800,000 individual reasoning steps of math solutions as correct, neutral or wrong. The resulting dataset, PRM800K (paper: "Let's Verify Step by Step"), showed that a reward model trained on steps picked correct solutions far more reliably than one trained only on final answers.

💡 Big idea An outcome reward model (ORM) scores only the end result, so every step shares the credit or blame. A process reward model (PRM) scores each step, pointing at exactly where reasoning went wrong. That's denser, more precise credit assignment, but step labels are expensive and PRMs can be gamed.
🧠 Analogy: marking the answer vs marking the working

One teacher only checks the final answer: a wrong answer gets 0, no explanation. Another marks every line of working and circles the exact line where it went wrong. The second teacher's feedback is far more useful, but takes much more effort, and a student could learn to write working that looks right to that teacher. That's outcome vs process rewards.

🎮 Playground · Where did the solution go wrong?

A 6-step solution that ends in a wrong answer. Click a step to set where the mistake happens. Below: the credit each method gives each step. The PRM here is estimated the "Math-Shepherd" way: for each step, run n random continuations from that point and count how often they still reach the right answer (a Monte Carlo estimate of V, Lesson 2.3).

The outcome reward says "everything was bad" for all six steps, including the good ones. The step-value estimate stays high until the mistake and then drops sharply. That drop is the step that should be blamed. With small n the estimates are noisy (try n = 2), which is the cost of labelling steps automatically.

Math refresher A PRM is a value function over reasoning prefixes

With reward 1 for a correct final answer, the value of a partial solution is V(prefix) = P(eventually correct | prefix). Read it as: "the probability that the answer ends up correct, given this partial solution so far" (the bar "|" means "given"). It's the value function from Lesson 2.3, applied to half-written solutions. A good step keeps V high; a mistake makes it drop. How good was one step? Compare the odds of success after it with the odds before it: step-level advantage ≈ V(after step) − V(before step), which is a TD error (Lesson 3.2) for reasoning.

✏️ Worked example: finding the bad step

So why didn't DeepSeek-R1 use one?

✂️

What's a "step"?

Reasoning doesn't come in neat steps. Splitting by line or sentence is arbitrary.

💸

Labels are expensive

Human step labels cost a lot. Automatic Monte Carlo labels need many rollouts per step.

🎣

Hackable

A neural PRM is a learned judge, so the policy can find steps it overrates (Lesson 8.6). The R1 paper cited exactly this.

Current practice: outcome rewards for RL training (cheap, hard to hack with a good verifier) and PRMs or verifiers for test-time search and Best-of-N selection. Getting dense credit without the downsides is one of the field's main open problems.

What "test-time search" looks like: write the first step 8 different ways, let the PRM score each, keep the best 2, extend each of those 8 ways, score again, and so on (a "beam search" over reasoning steps). A PRM that is too hackable to train against can still be a useful guide here, because nothing is being optimized against it for millions of steps. It's the same plan-by-search idea as MCTS (Lesson 6.3b) and a step-by-step version of Best-of-N (Lesson 7.7).

🌍 Where it's used
A PRM for test-time reranking 🧪 Research · 2025 Qwen2.5-Math-PRM (Alibaba, open weights)

How: state = problem + steps so far · value = the PRM's score for each step. At test time, sample N solutions and keep the one whose steps score best.

Why this method: step labels made by “try many completions and count wins” were noisy. Keeping only the steps where that estimate and an LLM judge agreed gave a better PRM.

Step scores guiding tree search 🧪 Research · 2025 rStar-Math (Microsoft)

How: state = problem + steps so far · action = next reasoning step · a small step-scoring model steers tree search (MCTS, Lesson 6.3b) toward promising steps.

Why this method: search lets a small model “think harder” without copying a bigger one: Qwen2.5-Math-7B went from 58.8% to 90.0% on MATH.

Process rewards inside GRPO 🧪 Research · 2024 DeepSeekMath

How: a PRM scores the end of every step (reward per step, normalized in the group). Each token's advantage = the sum of normalized step rewards from its step onward.

Why this method: dense credit says which step went wrong, and it beat outcome-only GRPO in their tests. Their later R1 still dropped it (see above) over cost and hacking.

🤯 Fun fact

In "Let's Verify Step by Step", using the process reward model to pick the best of many sampled solutions solved 78% of a representative subset of the MATH test set, clearly beating the outcome-supervised model. The paper argued process supervision is also safer, since it rewards a human-endorsed reasoning process rather than any path that happens to land on the answer.

🌉 Open research directions
  • Implicit PRMs: deriving step-level rewards from a model trained only on outcome labels (like DPO's implicit reward, 7.5). PRIME (2025) does this during RL: its step-scorer is updated online from rollouts and right/wrong outcomes, with no step labels at all.
  • Tree-structured rollouts: branch at uncertain tokens, compare siblings, and assign credit to the branching point.
  • Turn-level credit for multi-step agents (8.10), where "steps" are natural: each tool call.

Check yourself ✅

1. What's the main advantage of process rewards?
Outcome rewards blame every step equally; process rewards localize the error.
2. Math-Shepherd-style automatic step labels estimate a step's value by…
Monte Carlo estimation of V(prefix), noisy with few rollouts.
3. Why did DeepSeek-R1 stick to outcome rewards for RL?
A rule-based outcome check is crude but hard to fool, and it scales.
📝 TL;DR
  • ORM: score the final answer. PRM: score each step = a value function over reasoning prefixes.
  • PRMs give precise credit (PRM800K: 78% best-of-N on a MATH subset), but labels are costly and they can be hacked.
  • Today: outcome rewards for RL, PRMs and verifiers for test-time search. Dense credit is an open problem.
