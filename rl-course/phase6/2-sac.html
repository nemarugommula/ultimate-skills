Phase 6 · Lesson 2

Max-entropy RL: SAC

Get reward, but stay as random as you can while doing it. This idea turns out to be the same math as RLHF's KL penalty.

🧰 You'll need (quick reminders)
  • Softmax with temperature: turns scores into probabilities; higher temperature = flatter (Lesson 5.1).
  • Q(s, a) = how good each action is (Lesson 2.3).
🎯 Hook

In 2018, researchers at Berkeley and Google used Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) to teach a real four-legged robot, the Minitaur, to walk in about two hours of real-world practice, with no simulator. Sample efficiency and stability made it the go-to algorithm for continuous control.

💡 Big idea Change the goal: maximize reward plus a bonus for being unpredictable (the policy's entropy). The agent keeps exploring, keeps all the good options instead of collapsing onto one, and learns more robustly.
🧠 Analogy: the tightrope walker

A tightrope walker who practises only one rigid way of standing falls the moment the wind changes. One who keeps wobbling a little on purpose learns to recover from many positions. SAC pays the agent a small bonus for that "wobble" (randomness), so it keeps several good options alive and copes better when things change.

In words first: score = total reward + α × how unpredictable your choices were. Here's that sentence in symbols (𝔼 = "on average over many episodes", Σt = "add up over all time steps", H = entropy, defined just below, π(·|s) = "the policy's whole probability list in state s"):

J(π) = 𝔼[ Σt rt + α · H(π(·|st)) ] "reward + α × how random my action choice is". α = temperature: how much you value randomness.
Math refresher Entropy: a number for "how unpredictable"
H(p) = − Σa p(a) · log p(a) "the average surprise of what you'll pick"
  • Always picks one action (p = [1, 0, 0, 0]): H = 0. Totally predictable.
  • Fair coin (½, ½): H = log 2 ≈ 0.69.
  • Uniform over 4 actions: H = log 4 ≈ 1.39, the maximum for 4 choices.

With that objective, the best policy in a state has a neat closed form: a softmax over Q-values with temperature α, the same trick LLMs use to turn scores into word probabilities. Score each action by Q/α, exponentiate, then divide by the total so the probabilities add up to 1. (∝ means "proportional to: compute these numbers, then normalize them to sum to 1".)

π*(a|s) ∝ exp( Q(s,a) / α ) High-value actions get most of the probability, but good alternatives keep a real share.

α → 0: a greedy policy (all-in on ↑, entropy 0). Large α: nearly uniform. In between: ↑ and → share most of the probability, because they're both good. If something changes and ↑ stops working, the agent has already been practising →. Try 💥: with the default Q-values and α = 1, the policy was already picking → about 40% of the time, so right after the break it still averages Q ≈ 3.05. A greedy policy (α = 0.05) gets 0 until it re-explores.

✏️ Worked example: the same Q-values at two temperatures

Three actions with Q = 2.0, 1.9 and 0. The max-entropy policy is softmax(Q/α).

  • α = 1 (values randomness): e2.0 ≈ 7.39, e1.9 ≈ 6.69, e0 = 1 → probabilities ≈ 0.49, 0.44, 0.07. The two good actions share the probability; the bad one is rarely tried.
  • α = 0.1 (barely values randomness): ≈ 0.73, 0.27, 0.00. Much more decisive.

Plain "pick the max" would always choose the first action and never learn that the second is nearly as good. SAC keeps both alive.

SAC in one breath

  • Off-policy actor-critic with a replay buffer (sample-efficient, like DQN and DDPG).
  • Twin critics with the min (from TD3) and soft target networks.
  • Stochastic actor: outputs a mean and a spread, samples an action from that bell curve, then squashes it into the allowed range (e.g. [−2, 2]) with tanh. It's trained to maximize Q − α · log π, i.e. "high value, plus a bonus for unlikely (random) choices".
  • Critic target includes the entropy bonus: r + γ(min Qtarg(s', a') − α log π(a'|s')).
  • Modern SAC tunes α automatically to hit a target entropy.

Why log π, and where did H go? Suppose the actor picks an action it gave probability 0.25. Its surprise is −log 0.25 ≈ 1.39; an action it was sure of (probability 1) has surprise 0. So −log π(a) = "how surprising the chosen action was". Average that surprise over the actions the policy actually picks, and you get exactly the entropy from the refresher above (a ~ π reads "a drawn from π"):

𝔼a ~ π[ −log π(a|s) ] = H(π(·|s)) "the average surprise of the actions you pick is your entropy". So the −α log π in each sampled update is just +α·H, estimated one sample at a time.

🏎️ In the wild: Sony AI's GT Sophy (Nature, 2022) was trained with QR-SAC: SAC whose critic predicts the whole spread of possible returns instead of one average (distributional RL, Lesson 4.4). It beat four of the world's best Gran Turismo drivers head-to-head, and since late 2023 it has been a selectable opponent in Gran Turismo 7 on PS5.

🌍 Where it's used
Max-entropy actor-critic in a shipped game 🏭 In production · 2023 Sony AI: GT Sophy released to Gran Turismo 7 players

How: state = speed, tire loads and slip, the track edges ahead, nearby cars · action = steering, throttle and brake, 10 times a second · reward = fast progress and passing, minus penalties for contact and leaving the track.

Why this method: every lap is driven in the real game on a rack of 10–20 PlayStations, so off-policy replay reuses every lap, and the entropy bonus keeps it trying varied lines instead of locking onto one too early.

Sample-efficient learning on real hardware 🧪 Research · 2022 A Walk in the Park: a robot dog learns to walk in 20 minutes

How: state = body tilt and velocity, joint angles and velocities, foot contacts · action = target angles for 12 joints, 20 times a second · reward = steady while walking at the target speed, minus turning.

Why this method: no simulator, so every second of real walking counts. A SAC variant that does many critic updates per real step learned a gait in about 20 minutes, outdoors on grass and mulch.

Auto-tuned temperature α 🧪 Research · 2018 SAC paper: a 3-finger hand turns a valve from pixels

How: state = raw camera images + finger joint readings · action = target angles for 9 finger joints · reward = how close the valve is to its goal angle, read from a sensor the policy can't see.

Why this method: you can't sweep hyperparameters on a real robot. Tuning α automatically gave similar results across random seeds; the task took about 20 hours of real practice.

🤯 Fun fact

Max-entropy RL is related to a long-standing idea in physics and statistics: the principle of maximum entropy (E.T. Jaynes, 1957). Among all policies consistent with what you know (here: "get high reward"), pick the most noncommittal one. Don't be more certain than the evidence allows.

🌉 LLM bridge · this is RLHF's optimal policy

The entropy bonus is secretly a KL leash. KL(p ‖ q) measures how far distribution p is from q (0 = identical). For 4 actions, a policy with entropy 0.50 sits at KL = 1.39 − 0.50 = 0.89 from the uniform policy; a uniform policy (H = 1.39) sits at 0. In general (‖ reads "measured against"):

KL(π ‖ uniform) = log(number of actions) − H(π) "more entropy = closer to uniform". Rewarding +α·H is exactly penalizing −α·KL to the uniform policy.

RLHF keeps the same leash but ties it to a different post: it maximizes reward minus β · KL(π ‖ πref), keeping the policy close to the original model instead of close to uniform (Lesson 7.4). Its optimal policy has the same shape as SAC's:

π*(y|x) ∝ πref(y|x) · exp( r(x,y) / β )

A temperature-β softmax over rewards, weighted by the reference model. That one equation leads directly to DPO (Phase 7). And the reverse failure, entropy dropping toward zero during LLM RL training (entropy collapse), is a major problem in reasoning-model training (Phase 8).

Check yourself ✅

1. What does the entropy bonus reward?
H(π(·|s)) is high when probability is spread across actions. SAC trades a little reward for staying spread out.
2. In max-entropy RL, as the temperature α → 0, the policy becomes…
softmax(Q/α) with a tiny α puts all the mass on the best action. That's ordinary RL.
3. RLHF's KL-regularized optimal policy is…
The reference model reweighted by exponentiated reward: SAC's softmax with πref as the prior. DPO is derived from this.
📝 TL;DR
  • Objective: reward + α · entropy. Optimal policy = softmax(Q/α).
  • Benefits: exploration, robustness, keeps several good options.
  • SAC: off-policy, twin critics, stochastic tanh-Gaussian actor, auto-tuned α.
  • RLHF's optimum πref·exp(r/β) is the same idea, and it's the foundation of DPO.
