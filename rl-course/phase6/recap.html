🗺️ Phase 6 map: five assumptions, removed one at a time

Your DQN and PPO agents (Labs 4–5) quietly assumed five things. Each Phase 6 lesson removed one:

  • A short list of actions, or fresh data you can afford to throw away → 6.1 DDPG/TD3, 6.2 SAC.
  • Millions of cheap tries → 6.3 learn a model and practise in imagination; 6.3b plan by search before each move.
  • Being allowed to try at all → 6.4 offline RL: learn from a fixed log.
  • Stumbling on reward by luck → 6.5 exploration bonuses.
  • A hand-written reward or a fixed opponent → 6.6 self-play, 6.7 imitation and inverse RL.

And 6.8 asked whether nature solved it first: dopamine looks a lot like a TD error.

🧾 Cheat sheet

DDPG / TD3

Continuous actions: actor μ(s) trained by gradient ascent on Q(s,a). TD3 fixes overestimation with twin critics (min), a delayed actor and target smoothing.

SAC (max-entropy)

Maximize reward + α·entropy. Optimal policy = softmax(Q/α). Off-policy, twin critics, tanh-Gaussian actor, auto-tuned α. RLHF's optimum πref·exp(r/β) has the same form.

Model-based

Learn P and R, plan with imagined experience. Dyna-Q (n replays per real step), MuZero (learned model + tree search), Dreamer (training in imagination). Beware model exploitation and compounding errors. Simulators + domain randomization or teacher–student training cross the reality gap.

Planning by search (MCTS)

Before each move: select (PUCT = Q + c·P·√N(s)/(1+N(s,a)), UCB with a policy prior) → expand → evaluate (value net or rollout) → back up. Play the most-visited move. AlphaZero: train the policy on visit shares and the value on the winner, repeat (policy iteration). AlphaTensor, AlphaDev, AlphaProof.

Offline RL

Fixed dataset, no interaction. Distribution shift → overestimated unseen actions. Pessimism (CQL), in-sample learning (IQL), Decision Transformer. Off-policy evaluation re-weights logged outcomes by πnew/πold.

Exploration

Sparse rewards defeat ε-greedy. Intrinsic novelty rewards: counts (1/√N) + optimism, curiosity (beware noisy TVs), RND, Go-Explore.

Self-play / MARL

Automatic curriculum (AlphaZero, OpenAI Five). Latest policy can cycle; averages and opponent pools/leagues converge. Non-stationarity, team credit assignment.

RL in the brain

Dopamine bursts ≈ TD error ("better than expected"); predicted rewards → no burst, missing rewards → dip. Dopamine teaches the striatum (ventral ≈ critic, dorsal ≈ actor; Go/No-Go). Habits ≈ model-free, planning ≈ model-based, hippocampal replay ≈ experience replay.

Imitation

Behavior cloning compounds errors (∝ εT²). DAgger labels the learner's own states (∝ εT). Inverse RL / GAIL learn the reward. SFT = behavior cloning with exposure bias.

LLM links

Reward-model exploitation ≈ actor exploiting a critic. KL-regularized RLHF ≈ max-entropy RL. DPO is offline. SPIN and proposer–solver self-play. RL after SFT fixes exposure bias.

🗂️ Case files: five real problems, one phase later

The same five problems follow you through the course (meet them). Here's what this phase changes for each.

🌡️ The cooling plant

Now: learn a model from the plant's own logs ("this setting, in this weather, uses this much energy"), plan with it every few minutes, and only take actions that pass hard safety checks. Google's 2018 cooling system works roughly this way.

Still breaks: a 30% saving in one season could be mild weather or luck, so you still need a way to prove the saving is real.

📱 The feed

Now: before shipping a new feed policy, estimate its value from the old policy's logs by re-weighting them, and train it pessimistically so it doesn't bet on items the logs barely show.

Still breaks: clicks and watch time are only a stand-in for what users want, and a strong optimizer will find content that games the stand-in (clickbait).

🦾 The robot hand

Now: an off-policy actor-critic for continuous motor commands (TD3/SAC-style) reuses every real try; most practice happens in a randomized simulator, starting from human demonstrations and large offline logs (QT-Opt learned from 580,000 logged grasps).

Still breaks: each policy learns one skill with one hand-written reward; asking it in plain words to “put the red cup in the sink” needs something new.

♟️ The board game

Now: self-play supplies opponents at your level; MCTS with PUCT checks each move ahead; the network learns to copy the search's visit shares and predict the winner, and the loop repeats. That's AlphaZero.

✅ Where it stands: solved for perfect-information games like Go and chess, from the rules alone. What's left is measuring strength fairly, over many games.

🧑‍💻 The coding agent

Now: start with behavior cloning: fine-tune the model on human-written code and bug fixes (that's SFT).

Still breaks: copying compounds errors once it goes off-script and caps it at the quality of the demos; it needs a reward for its own attempts, from people's judgments or from running the tests.

🧩 Frame it yourself

🧩 New problem

A warehouse runs 200 small robots that carry shelves to packing stations. Today a hand-written planner routes them, and you have a year of its logs plus a simulator of the floor. Management wants more orders packed per hour, without collisions or robots jamming the aisles.

Before you open the answer, write down: the state, the action, the reward, episodic or continuing (and γ), and which method from this phase (or earlier) you'd try first.

Reveal One good framing + the classic mistakes

State: for each robot, its position, heading, goal and battery, plus the positions and headings of nearby robots. Action: move to the next cell, turn, or wait, every tick. Reward: a shared team reward: +1 per shelf delivered, a large penalty for any collision, a small penalty per tick spent blocking an aisle. Episodic? In reality it's continuing (the warehouse never stops), so use γ ≈ 0.99; in simulation you can cut it into shifts.

Method: first behavior-clone the planner from its logs, so you start safe and competent (the planner is your expert). Then improve with RL in the simulator, with every robot running the same shared policy, self-play style: the traffic gets smarter as you do. Before going live, check it on held-out simulated days and keep the planner as a fallback.

Classic mistakes: rewarding each robot only for its own deliveries (robots learn to block each other: a team credit-assignment problem); leaving other robots out of the state (it's no longer Markov, and the world looks random); trusting the simulator blindly (wheel slip and people on the floor are the reality gap); and running plain Q-learning on the planner's logs, which only show the planner's choices, so untried moves get wildly overrated.

🔬 Detail worth noticing in the SAC code

The actor samples with the reparameterization trick (a = tanh(μ + σ·noise)), so gradients flow through the sampled action into the actor's weights. It also applies a tanh correction to the log-probability. Forgetting that correction is a classic silent bug.

Boss quiz 👾

You must learn a robot policy from 2 years of logged factory data, with no new experiments allowed. What's your biggest risk, and the remedy?
Distribution shift is the central offline RL failure. Pessimism keeps the policy where the data is.
SAC with temperature α → 0 behaves most like…
No entropy bonus means plain reward maximization. The softmax(Q/α) policy becomes greedy.
An LLM fine-tuned only with SFT writes well, but once it makes an early mistake in a long proof, it rarely recovers. Which idea explains this?
SFT only ever saw perfect prefixes. RL on its own outputs (like DAgger) teaches recovery.
