Phase 5 · Lesson 4

SLAM in practice: ICP, lidar SLAM & ORB-SLAM

How do two lidar scans, taken a moment apart, tell a robot how far it moved? The answer is a beautifully simple loop called ICP. Then a tour of the real systems built on it and on camera features (LOAM, LIO-SAM, KISS-ICP, Cartographer, slam_toolbox, ORB-SLAM), and how to choose one for your robot.

🧰 You'll need (quick reminders)
  • A 2D rigid motion = a rotation by θ plus a shift (tx, ty): R p + t (Lessons 1.2 and 1.5).
  • Pose graphs: SLAM back-ends that turn “I moved this much” and “I've been here” edges into a consistent map (Lesson 5.3). This lesson is about the front-end that produces those edges.
  • SVD (singular value decomposition) splits a matrix into rotation-stretch-rotation; here it pulls the best rotation out of a 2×2 matrix. Refresher: SVD in the Math course. ORB features and RANSAC are Lessons 4.1 and 4.2.
🎯 Hook · a puzzle first

A robot takes a lidar scan, drives a bit, and takes another. Same room, seen from a new spot: 180 points each. How far did it move, and how much did it turn?

If someone told you which point in scan 2 is the same wall spot as which point in scan 1, this would be a one-line formula. Nobody tells you. Here's the cheeky trick that solves it: pretend the nearest point is the match, compute the motion, apply it, and do it again. It sounds like it shouldn't work. It usually does, within a few dozen rounds.

That trick is ICP, Iterative Closest Point, published by Paul Besl and Neil McKay (IEEE TPAMI, 1992) and, in a point-to-plane flavour, by Yang Chen and Gérard Medioni (ICRA 1991). In 2023, a team from the University of Bonn showed that plain point-to-point ICP, “done the right way”, rivals much more complex lidar odometry systems: KISS-ICP (arXiv).

ICP in two steps

  1. Match: for every point of the new scan (placed at the current guess), find the closest point in the old scan.
  2. Align: find the rotation and shift that best line up those pairs (least squares, solved exactly with an SVD). Apply it. Go back to 1 until nothing changes.
🧩 Predict: in the playground below the robot really turned 14° and moved about 1 m. Starting ICP from “no motion at all” (a 14° error), will it find the answer? What about starting from a guess that's 90° wrong?
From “no motion” it converges, in about 20 iterations. From 90° off it locks onto a wrong answer and stops there, perfectly content: every point has a nearby partner, just the wrong one. In this room, starting guesses from roughly −65° to +70° work and beyond that ICP fails. That's a local minimum, and it's why ICP is always started from an odometry or IMU guess.

✏️ Worked example: the align step with three pairs

Old points A: (0, 0), (2, 0), (0, 1). Their matches B: (1, 1), (1, 3), (0, 1). What rotation and shift take A onto B?

  1. Centroids: A's is (0.67, 0.33), B's is (0.67, 1.67). Subtract them so both sets are centred.
  2. Cross-covariance: add up products of centred coordinates: Sxx = Σ axbx = 0.67, Sxy = Σ axby = 2.67, Syx = −0.67, Syy = −0.67.
  3. Rotation: in 2D the SVD has a shortcut, θ = atan2(Sxy − Syx, Sxx + Syy) = atan2(3.33, 0) = 90°.
  4. Shift: t = (centroid of B) − R × (centroid of A) = (0.67, 1.67) − (−0.33, 0.67) = (1, 1).

Check: rotating (2, 0) by 90° gives (0, 2), plus (1, 1) is (1, 3). ✓ In 3D you need the full SVD of the 3×3 matrix (Arun, Huang & Blostein, TPAMI 1987), but the recipe is the same: centre, multiply, decompose.

💡 Big idea ICP swaps an impossible problem (find the matches and the motion) for two easy ones it alternates between: matches given motion (nearest neighbours) and motion given matches (closed-form SVD). Each round can only lower the error, so it always settles, but only to the nearest valley. A good starting guess is not optional.
🤔 Why this? (and not something simpler)
  1. The problem before. Wheel odometry slips on smooth floors and gravel, and an IMU drifts (Lesson 2.5). A lidar sees the world's geometry directly, but two scans are just clouds of points with no labels saying which point is which.
  2. What people tried. Extract features (corners, line segments) and match those (fragile: smooth walls have no corners, and clutter makes fake ones). Brute-force search over every rotation and shift (exact but slow: 360 angles × 100 × 100 shifts = 3.6 million poses to score per scan pair).
  3. Why this works. Nearest-neighbour matching is cheap with a k-d tree, and for given matches the best rigid motion has an exact closed form. Alternate them: in the playground, “no motion” → the true 14° turn and 1 m shift in about 20 rounds; the worked example's three pairs give 90° and (1, 1) by hand.
  4. What it costs, when it fails. It converges to the nearest minimum: start too far off (beyond about 65–70° here) and it locks onto a wrong answer. Outliers (people, cars, points seen in only one scan) drag it off unless you cap the match distance. Featureless places (long corridors, tunnels, open fields) leave a direction unconstrained, so it slides (Lesson 5.6). Moving while scanning distorts the scan (“motion skew”) unless you correct it.
  5. What came next. Point-to-plane ICP (match to the local surface, converges faster on walls), NDT (match to local Gaussian blobs, Lesson 5.6), feature-based lidar odometry (LOAM: edges and planes), tightly coupled lidar + IMU (LIO-SAM: the IMU de-skews scans and gives the starting guess), and KISS-ICP (point-to-point with adaptive thresholds and a robust kernel). Global methods (scan context, branch-and-bound in Cartographer) find loop closures without any guess.

Outliers: the people in your scan

Real scans contain things the other scan doesn't: a person who walked in, a door that opened, reflections. Those points still get a “nearest neighbour”, usually a wrong one, and they pull the answer. The standard fix is a maximum correspondence distance: ignore any pair farther apart than d. In Open3D that's the max_correspondence_distance argument. Too small, and from a rough start nothing matches; too big, and every outlier votes.

🧩 Predict: four people are standing in the new scan only. With no distance limit at all, will the outliers make ICP fail completely, or just make it a bit wrong?
Just wrong, and that's worse, because it looks fine. In the playground below the unlimited version settles about 40 cm off, with a respectable-looking average match distance. Nothing flags it. Capping the distance at around 0.5–1 m brings the error down to a few centimetres.

From ICP to full systems

A lidar SLAM system is ICP (or a cousin) for odometry, plus a map to match against, plus loop closure and a pose graph (Lesson 5.3). The main families, all verified against their papers and repositories (Nov 2026):

SystemSensorsFront-end ideaStatus / licence
LOAM (Zhang & Singh, RSS 2014)3D lidarSplit SLAM in two: fast, rough odometry at a high rate, and fine registration to the map at a rate “an order of magnitude lower”; matches edge and plane pointsPaper; many open re-implementations
LIO-SAM (Shan et al., IROS 2020)3D lidar + IMU (+ GPS)Factor graph (GTSAM): IMU pre-integration de-skews scans and gives the initial guess; lidar odometry estimates the IMU bias; loop closures and GPS as factorsOpen source, BSD-3
KISS-ICP (Vizzo et al., RA-L 2023)any 3D lidarPoint-to-point ICP with adaptive matching threshold, a robust kernel, simple motion compensation and subsampling; few parametersOpen source, MIT; pip install kiss-icp (odometry only)
Cartographer (Hess et al., ICRA 2016)2D/3D lidar (+ IMU)Scans inserted into local submaps; loop closures by fast branch-and-bound scan-to-submap matching; 5 cm maps in real time from a backpackGoogle open source; README says it is no longer actively maintained
slam_toolbox (Macenski & Jambrecic, 2021)2D lidarScan matching + pose graph (Ceres by default); mapping, continuing and merging maps, localization modeThe SLAM library Nav2's docs recommend for ROS 2

Cameras need a different front-end: instead of every point, track distinctive features. ORB-SLAM2 (Mur-Artal & Tardós, 2017) does monocular, stereo and RGB-D SLAM with ORB features (Lesson 4.1), bundle adjustment (4.7), loop closing with a bag of words (5.3) and relocalization. ORB-SLAM3 (Campos et al., 2021) adds tightly integrated visual-inertial SLAM and a multi-map system: when it gets lost it starts a new map and merges it with the old one when it recognizes a place; its paper reports 2 to 5 times better accuracy than previous approaches. Both are GPLv3, which matters for commercial products.

🧰 Tools you'll actually use

The align step in NumPy (the Phase 5 lab has you write the whole 2D ICP), then Open3D for 3D point clouds (version 0.20 on PyPI as of Nov 2026), KISS-ICP for quick lidar odometry, and slam_toolbox on ROS 2.

import numpy as np

def best_fit(P, Q):
    """Rotation R and shift t minimizing Σ |R p + t − q|² for matched rows of P and Q (N×2 or N×3)."""
    cp, cq = P.mean(axis=0), Q.mean(axis=0)
    U, S, Vt = np.linalg.svd((P - cp).T @ (Q - cq))     # SVD of the cross-covariance
    R = Vt.T @ U.T
    if np.linalg.det(R) < 0:                             # a reflection sneaked in: flip the last axis
        Vt[-1] *= -1; R = Vt.T @ U.T
    return R, cq - R @ cp
import numpy as np, open3d as o3d
reg = o3d.pipelines.registration
source = o3d.io.read_point_cloud("scan_new.pcd")
target = o3d.io.read_point_cloud("scan_old.pcd")
init = np.eye(4)                                        # in practice: the odometry / IMU guess
result = reg.registration_icp(
    source, target, 0.5, init,                           # max_correspondence_distance = 0.5 m
    reg.TransformationEstimationPointToPoint(),
    reg.ICPConvergenceCriteria(max_iteration=50))
print(result.fitness, result.inlier_rmse)               # share of points matched, and their RMS error
T = result.transformation                               # 4×4 pose of the new scan in the old scan's frame
# point-to-plane: target.estimate_normals(), then reg.TransformationEstimationPointToPlane()
# KISS-ICP lidar odometry on a folder of scans or a ROS bag

🚗 How Tesla / Waymo / drones do it

🚕 Waymo

Waymo's cars carry lidar and localize against detailed prior maps; a 2016 Waymo post says the map lets the car know where it is “to within 10cm of accuracy”. Matching each live lidar sweep to a prior map is the scan-to-map version of this lesson (Lesson 5.6). Waymo hasn't published which registration method it uses.

Sources: Waymo, Dec 2016, Waymo, Aug 2026

🚘 Tesla

Tesla's production cars have no lidar, so lidar ICP isn't part of its driving stack. Tesla hasn't published a visual SLAM system either; its public talks describe networks that turn video from all cameras into the road layout and 3D occupancy (Lessons 6.2, 6.3).

Sources: tesla.com/AI

🛸 Drones

Lidar is heavy and power-hungry for small drones, so they mostly use cameras + IMU: visual-inertial odometry and SLAM in the ORB-SLAM3 family. Skydio's X10 page describes six custom navigation lenses giving 360° visibility for its vision-based autonomy.

Sources: Skydio X10, ORB-SLAM3

🤖 Robots

Indoor ROS 2 robots: 2D lidar + slam_toolbox, then Nav2 localization on the saved map. Outdoor robots with 3D lidar: LIO-SAM-style lidar-inertial SLAM, or KISS-ICP odometry under a separate back-end.

Sources: slam_toolbox, KISS-ICP

Why they differ: lidar gives geometry directly and works in the dark, which suits cars that can carry it and robots that need reliability; ICP-style matching is its natural partner. Cameras are light and cheap, the right trade on a drone, but need feature tracking and an IMU to survive fast motion. Tesla's bet is that learned perception from cameras can replace both the lidar and the prior map.

🎤 Interview angle

“Walk me through ICP and tell me how it fails.” A strong answer:

  • Two alternating steps: nearest-neighbour matches (k-d tree), then closed-form alignment (SVD, with the reflection fix).
  • Failure modes: bad initial guess → local minimum; outliers → biased answer (max correspondence distance, robust kernels); degenerate geometry (corridors) → slides along the unconstrained direction; motion skew → de-skew with the IMU.
  • Point-to-plane vs point-to-point; NDT as an alternative; how a system (LIO-SAM, KISS-ICP, slam_toolbox) wraps ICP with an IMU guess, a local map and loop closures.
  • Can pick a system for a given robot and justify it by sensors, compute, environment and licence.
🏭 In the real world
ICP 📜 History · 1992 Besl & McKay, TPAMI

How: register 3D shapes by repeatedly matching closest points and solving for the best rigid motion.

Why this method: no features or labels needed, and each iteration has an exact solution.

Real-time loop closure from a backpack 🏢 Google · 2016 Hess et al., ICRA 2016

How: Cartographer builds submaps from lidar scans and finds loop closures with a branch-and-bound search of scan-to-submap matches, producing 5 cm maps in real time.

Why this method: branch-and-bound finds the best match over a large search window without a good initial guess, which ICP can't.

LIO-SAM 🧰 Open source Shan et al., IROS 2020

How: lidar odometry, IMU pre-integration, GPS and loop closures as factors in one GTSAM factor graph.

Why this method: the IMU fixes motion skew and gives ICP a great starting guess; the factor graph fuses everything consistently.

ORB-SLAM3 🧪 Research · 2021 Campos et al., T-RO

How: ORB features, visual-inertial MAP estimation, bag-of-words place recognition and a multi-map atlas.

Why this method: cameras and IMUs are cheap and light; multi-map handling lets it survive losing track and merge maps later.

🤯 Fun fact · Google's SLAM, mapped on foot

Cartographer's paper demonstrates real-time mapping and loop closure on a backpack carried through buildings, producing maps at 5 cm resolution with limited onboard computing (Google Research). The project later became one of the most popular open-source SLAM systems; as of Nov 2026 its README says it is no longer actively maintained, a good reminder to check a project's pulse before building a product on it.

Check yourself

1. Two centred point sets give Sxx = 3, Syy = 3, Sxy = 0, Syx = 0. What rotation does the 2D align step return?
θ = atan2(Sxy − Syx, Sxx + Syy) = atan2(0, 6) = 0°. The centred coordinates already line up; only a shift remains (difference of the centroids).
2. Your robot's ICP odometry is great in the lab but in a busy station hall it reports the robot drifting sideways while it's standing still. Most likely cause and fix?
Crowds are outliers that appear in one scan and not the next, or move between scans. Every one gets a nearest neighbour and pulls the answer. A max correspondence distance and robust kernels (as in KISS-ICP) limit their pull; removing points classified as people helps further.
3. A 250 g indoor drone, no GPS, must map a warehouse. Which setup fits best?
A 3D lidar alone can weigh more than the whole drone. Cameras and an IMU weigh grams, work in 3D, and give place recognition for loop closure. slam_toolbox assumes a robot moving on a flat floor with a horizontal 2D lidar.
📝 TL;DR
  • ICP: match each point to its nearest neighbour, solve the best rigid motion in closed form (SVD), repeat.
  • 2D shortcut: θ = atan2(Sxy − Syx, Sxx + Syy), t = centroidB − R centroidA.
  • Needs a good starting guess (local minima) and a max match distance (outliers); corridors make it slide.
  • Systems: LOAM (edges/planes), LIO-SAM (lidar + IMU factor graph), KISS-ICP (simple, few parameters), Cartographer (unmaintained), slam_toolbox (ROS 2 default choice), ORB-SLAM2/3 (cameras, GPLv3).
  • Waymo matches lidar to prior maps; drones use cameras + IMU; Tesla hasn't published a SLAM system.
