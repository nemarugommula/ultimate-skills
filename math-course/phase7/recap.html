Phase 7 · Recap

Recap: optimization

Training a model = finding the best knobs. You now know the five pieces of every modern training recipe: the loss, the mini-batch, the optimizer and its schedule, the regularizers, and the constraints.

🧾 Cheat sheet

⛰️

7.1 Loss & landscapes

MSE = mean(e²) · MAE = mean(|e|) · Huber: ½e² small, linear big

One number for “how wrong”; its slope sets each error's pull. Train on smooth losses (log-loss), not accuracy. Landscapes: bowls, local minima, saddles, plateaus.

Used in: regression (least squares), DQN's clipped error, LLM cross-entropy, loss-landscape plots.

🎲

7.2 SGD & mini-batches

g = (1/B) Σbatch ∇ℓᵢ,  w ← w − ηg

A random handful gives a noisy but unbiased slope. Noise ∝ 1/√B. Epoch = N/B steps. Batch × k → η × k (linear scaling).

Used in: every LLM (GPT-3: 3.2M tokens/step), data-parallel GPUs, DQN replay, PPO epochs.

🏎️

7.3 Momentum, Adam, schedules

m ← βm + g · Adam: w −= η m̂/(√v̂ + ε)

Momentum lets agreeing gradients add up. Adam gives each weight a step of about η. Warmup, then cosine decay.

Used in: LLaMA/GPT-3 (AdamW, β₂ = 0.95, cosine to 10%), Transformer warmup (4,000 steps), PPO (Adam).

🧯

7.4 Regularization

L + λΣw²  ·  L + λΣ|w|

Weight decay shrinks weights each step; L1 makes exact zeros; dropout zeroes neurons with probability p (survivors × 1/(1 − p)); early stopping; augmentation.

Used in: LLM weight decay 0.1, Transformer dropout 0.1, AlexNet crops/flips, lasso feature selection.

⚖️

7.5 Constraints & Lagrange

∇f = λ∇g · ℒ = f − λ(g − c)

At the best constrained point the goal's contour just touches the constraint. λ = the price: gain per extra unit of budget. Penalty ⇔ limit.

Used in: SVM support vectors, TRPO's KL limit, PPO's clip and adaptive β, RLHF's β.

🗣️ Teach it back

Explain to a friend, in under two minutes and without symbols: “How does an LLM actually get trained?” Try to use all five ideas: what number it's trying to shrink (and why not accuracy), why it only looks at a small batch per step, why Adam plus a warmup-then-cosine learning rate, what weight decay and dropout protect against, and how RLHF's β is a price for drifting from the original model.

If you get stuck on one, that's the lesson to revisit.

🧩 Use it yourself: plan a fine-tuning run

✏️ The task

You fine-tune a small model on 60,000 examples with batch size 100 for 5 epochs, using AdamW. The learning rate warms up linearly for 300 steps to a peak of 1 × 10⁻³, then follows a cosine down to 10% of the peak at the last step. Weight decay λ = 0.1.

  1. How many training steps in total?
  2. What's the learning rate at step 150? At step 1,650?
  3. At the peak learning rate, AdamW multiplies every weight by what factor per step (from weight decay alone)?
  4. You get a second GPU and double the batch to 200. Using the linear scaling rule, what are the new peak learning rate and total steps?
Reveal The worked solution
  1. Steps per epoch = 60,000 / 100 = 600. Total = 600 × 5 = 3,000 steps (Lesson 7.2).
  2. Step 150 is halfway up the warmup: 10⁻³ × 150/300 = 5 × 10⁻⁴. Step 1,650 is (1,650 − 300) / (3,000 − 300) = 1,350/2,700 = halfway through the cosine, where cos(π/2) = 0, so η = 10⁻⁴ + ½ × (10⁻³ − 10⁻⁴) × 1 = 5.5 × 10⁻⁴: the average of peak and floor (Lesson 7.3).
  3. AdamW's decay step is w ← w − ηλw, so the factor is 1 − 10⁻³ × 0.1 = 0.9999. Tiny per step, but over 1,000 such steps it compounds to 0.9999¹⁰⁰⁰ ≈ 0.905: about a 10% shrink unless the data pushes back (Lesson 7.4).
  4. Batch × 2 → peak η × 2 = 2 × 10⁻³. Steps per epoch halve to 300, so 1,500 steps in total (and you'd halve the warmup to 150 steps to keep it the same share of training). Each step averages the two GPUs' gradients, exactly like one batch of 200 (Lesson 7.2).

🏆 Boss quiz

1. Errors [0.5, 3]. What is the average Huber loss with δ = 1?
0.5 is small: ½ × 0.25 = 0.125. 3 is big: 1 × (3 − 0.5) = 2.5. Average (0.125 + 2.5)/2 = 1.3125. MSE would be (0.25 + 9)/2 = 4.625 and MAE 1.75.
2. You raise the batch size from 64 to 256. Roughly how does the noise in each gradient estimate change?
Noise shrinks like 1/√B. 4× the batch → √4 = 2 → half the noise, for 4× the cost per step.
3. Which optimizer property explains why one weight with gradients near 1,000 and another near 0.001 move by similar amounts?
m̂/√v̂ is about ±1 for any weight with consistent gradients, so each step is about η. Momentum alone would still move the first weight a million times further.
4. A lasso model on 300 features gives 280 coefficients of exactly 0. What caused that?
The slope of λ|w| is ±λ whatever the weight's size, so weak features get shoved to exactly 0: automatic feature selection. L2 would only make them small.
5. TRPO limits KL(old ‖ new) ≤ δ. PPO's adaptive-KL version instead subtracts β · KL and doubles or halves β to hit a target KL. In Lagrange terms, what is PPO doing?
A hard limit and a penalty agree when the penalty's price equals the limit's Lagrange multiplier. Since that price isn't known in advance, PPO tunes β on the fly from the measured KL.
