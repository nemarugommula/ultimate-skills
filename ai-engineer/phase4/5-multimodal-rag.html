Phase 4 · Lesson 5

Multimodal RAG: images, tables & visual documents

Real documents aren't just text. The answer is often in a chart, a table, a diagram or a scanned page, and a text-only pipeline never sees it.

🧰 You'll need (quick reminders)
  • Ingestion: parsing PDFs, HTML and tables into text; OCR (optical character recognition) turns pictures of text into text (Lesson 3.2).
  • Late interaction: one vector per token, scored with MaxSim (ColBERT, Lesson 4.2).
  • Vision-language models (VLMs) read images and answer questions about them (Lesson 8.4 goes deeper).
🎯 Hook

The standard way to index a PDF is a pipeline: detect the layout, OCR the text, ask a vision model to caption every figure, then embed the text. In 2024 a team from Illuin Technology, Equall and CentraleSupélec measured it at 7.22 seconds per page to index. Then they tried skipping it all: feed a screenshot of each page to a vision-language model and store its patch vectors. That took 0.39 seconds per page.

And it retrieved better. On their visual document benchmark, the best text pipeline (OCR + captions + a strong text embedder) scored 67.0 nDCG@5; the screenshot model, ColPali, scored 81.3.

Numbers first: where answers hide

Take a typical 40-page quarterly report. A text-only pipeline reads the PDF's text layer and nothing else.

Where the fact livesWhat a text-only index seesResult

In decks, financial filings, manuals and research papers, a large share of the key facts live in charts and tables. If your eval questions only ask about prose, you'll never notice the hole.

💡 Big idea There are two ways to make visual content searchable. Convert it to text (OCR, table-to-Markdown, VLM captions) and reuse your text pipeline, or embed the visuals directly (multimodal embeddings, or page screenshots with ColPali-style late interaction) and give the answering model the image. Conversion is mature and cheap to query; direct embedding keeps what conversion loses (layout, charts, handwriting) and skips the brittle parsing.
🧠 Analogy: describing a photo vs showing it

You can phone a friend and describe a chart (“the APAC bar is tallest, about 11%”), or send them a photo. Describing is quick to search later and cheap to send, but you'll only describe what you thought mattered. The photo keeps everything, and they can read off details you never mentioned, but it's bigger to store and send. Captioning is describing; page-image retrieval is sending the photo.

Why not just CLIP? CLIP (OpenAI, 2021) learned from 400 million image-caption pairs from the internet, so it's great at “a dog on a beach” and poor at reading a dense page. On ColPali's benchmark, a CLIP-style model (Jina-CLIP) averaged 17.7 nDCG@5, against 81.3 for ColPali.

Per-page indexing times are the ColPali paper's measurements of an Unstructured pipeline (layout 0.81 s, OCR 2.67 s, captioning 3.71 s, encoding 0.03 s) and of ColPali (0.39 s), on their hardware; yours will differ. Image tokens use Claude's published rule: a 1000×1000 page ≈ 1,296 tokens.

What you should see: text-only answers one question of five. Each conversion fixes its own kind of page: OCR the scan, Markdown the table, captions the chart. Turn on all three and you get five of five, at about 7 seconds of indexing per page. Page-image retrieval alone also gets five of five, about 18× faster to index, but it sends page images (≈1,300 tokens each) to a model that must read them.

✏️ Worked example: 100,000 pages, two pipelines
  • Parse + OCR + caption + text embedding at 7.22 s/page: 100,000 × 7.22 = 722,000 s ≈ 200 hours on one worker (parallelise to taste), plus a VLM call per figure for captions. Storage: one 1,024-dim float32 vector per chunk ≈ 4 KB; for 100,000 page-chunks ≈ 0.4 GB.
  • ColPali at 0.39 s/page: 100,000 × 0.39 = 39,000 s ≈ 11 hours. Storage: 1,030 vectors × 128 dims × 2 bytes = 263,680 bytes ≈ 257.5 KB per page → ≈ 26 GB, about 64× more, before compression or token pooling.
  • At question time: top-3 text chunks ≈ 1,500 tokens; top-3 page images ≈ 3 × 1,296 = 3,888 tokens, roughly 2.6× more input per question, and the answering model must be a capable vision model.

Neither wins on every axis: conversion is expensive once, image retrieval costs more storage and more per question. Many teams do both: text for search and citations, page images for the answering model when the page is visual.

🧰 Tools you'll actually use

Parsing: Docling (IBM, open source: layout, reading order, table structure, OCR), Unstructured, LlamaParse, cloud document AI services. Visual retrieval: ColPali / ColQwen models (on Hugging Face under vidore), the byaldi wrapper, Vespa and Qdrant for multivector storage. Multimodal embeddings: Voyage multimodal-3 (interleaved text and screenshots), Cohere Embed, CLIP-family models for photos. Answering: any vision-capable LLM.

# Convert: PDF → Markdown with tables kept as tables
from docling.document_converter import DocumentConverter
md = DocumentConverter().convert("q3-report.pdf").document.export_to_markdown()

# Embed the visuals: page screenshots with ColQwen2 via byaldi
from byaldi import RAGMultiModalModel
rag = RAGMultiModalModel.from_pretrained("vidore/colqwen2-v1.0")
rag.index(input_path="reports/", index_name="reports", store_collection_with_index=False, overwrite=True)
hits = rag.search("Which region grew fastest in Q3?", k=3)   # → doc_id, page_num, score

# Answer: send the retrieved page image to a vision model
import base64
from anthropic import Anthropic
img = base64.b64encode(open(page_png_path(hits[0]), "rb").read()).decode()
msg = Anthropic().messages.create(model="claude-sonnet-5-5", max_tokens=300, messages=[{"role": "user", "content": [
    {"type": "image", "source": {"type": "base64", "media_type": "image/png", "data": img}},
    {"type": "text", "text": "Using only this page, which region grew fastest in Q3? Quote the value."}]}])

page_png_path is your own helper that renders or looks up the page image (byaldi can store base64 pages with store_collection_with_index=True, at a big storage cost).

🚀 Startup vs big tech

Week one

Run a good parser (Docling or a hosted one) that keeps tables as Markdown, OCR anything without a text layer, and add 20 eval questions whose answers live in charts and tables. If your corpus is slides or reports, try a ColPali-style index on a sample before building a captioning pipeline.

At scale

Per-document-type ingestion lanes, a document-AI service for scans and forms, captions generated in batch with cheap models, a visual index for slide-heavy sources, and quality monitors on parser output (empty pages, garbled tables) because parsing failures are silent until a customer finds them.

🎤 Interview angle

“Our RAG over financial reports can't answer questions about charts and tables. Fix it.” A senior answer:

  • Diagnoses with an eval split by where the answer lives (text, table, chart, scan) instead of one average.
  • Tables: structure-preserving extraction to Markdown/HTML, row-aware chunks with headers repeated, or load them into SQL for aggregates (4.6).
  • Charts and scans: captions with values via a VLM, or page-image retrieval (ColPali) with a vision model answering; compares indexing time, storage and per-question tokens.
  • Keeps citations to page numbers, and checks extracted numbers against the source image for high-stakes answers.
🏭 In the real world
Retrieve page screenshots 🧪 Research · 2024 ColPali (Illuin Technology, Equall, CentraleSupélec)

How: a PaliGemma-3B vision-language model outputs 128-dim vectors for each of 1,024 image patches; queries match them with ColBERT-style MaxSim.

Why this method: text pipelines are slow and lose visual cues. ColPali averaged 81.3 nDCG@5 on ViDoRe versus 67.0 for OCR + captioning + BGE-M3, and indexed a page in 0.39 s versus 7.22 s.

Visual retrieval in a search engine 🏢 Vespa · 2024 PDF retrieval with vision language models

How: stores ColPali patch embeddings per page and computes MaxSim inside the engine, next to BM25 and metadata filters.

Why this method: brings page-image retrieval into an engine that also does BM25, filters and hybrid ranking, so teams don't run a separate system.

One embedding for screenshots and text 🏢 Voyage AI · 2024 voyage-multimodal-3

How: a single model vectorises interleaved text and images, including screenshots of PDFs, slides, tables and figures.

Why this method: removes complex document parsing; Voyage reports a 19.63% average retrieval improvement over the next best multimodal embedding model across 20 datasets.

Open-source document conversion 🧰 Open source Docling (IBM Research)

How: layout analysis, reading order, table structure recognition, OCR and formulas, exported as Markdown, HTML or JSON.

Why this method: the convert-to-text path lives or dies on parsing quality, especially tables; a local, open parser keeps sensitive documents on your servers.

🤯 Fun fact · a page is worth ~1,300 tokens

Claude counts images in 28×28-pixel patches: a 1000×1000 page image costs ⌈1000/28⌉ × ⌈1000/28⌉ = 36 × 36 = 1,296 tokens, whatever is on it. A blank page and a page of tiny print cost the same, while the extracted text of a dense page can easily exceed that and a sparse slide might be 50 tokens. That's why many systems send text when the page is text-heavy and the image when it's visual.

Check yourself

1. 20,000 slide pages. ColPali stores ≈ 257.5 KB per page; a text pipeline stores one 4 KB vector per page. Roughly how much storage does each need?
20,000 × 257.5 KB ≈ 5.15 GB versus 20,000 × 4 KB = 80 MB: about 64× more. Fine for 20,000 pages; at 100 million pages it's a design problem, which is why compression and token pooling research exists.
2. Your insurance-claims RAG answers questions about typed policy documents well, but fails on customers' scanned, handwritten claim forms. What do you fix first?
A scan is a picture; without OCR or visual retrieval there is nothing to embed, so no retrieval setting can find it. Always monitor ingestion for empty or near-empty pages.
3. Analysts ask “what was the average deal size per region per quarter?” over hundreds of spreadsheet-like tables in PDFs. Best design?
That's an aggregate across many rows: retrieval returns a few pages, but the average needs all of them, computed exactly. Structured data wants a structured query (Lesson 4.6).
📝 TL;DR
  • Text-only pipelines are blind to charts and scans and scramble tables: evaluate by where the answer lives.
  • Convert (OCR, tables → Markdown, VLM captions) to reuse text search; or embed visuals directly (multimodal embeddings, ColPali page images).
  • ColPali: 81.3 vs 67.0 nDCG@5 and 0.39 vs 7.22 s per page to index, at ~64× the storage and image tokens per question.
  • Big tables with aggregate questions belong in SQL, not in a vector index.
