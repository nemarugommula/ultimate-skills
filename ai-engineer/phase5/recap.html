Phase 5 · Recap

Recap: Evaluation

Datasets with error bars, metrics that measure the right property, judges you've calibrated, RAG and agent evals by layer, and a loop from CI to production and back. Here's the phase on one page, a game to sharpen your instincts, and an eval plan to design yourself.

🧾 Cheat sheet

🎯

5.1 Why evals

95% error bar ≈ ±1.96·√(p(1−p)/n): ±17.5 pts at n = 20, ±2.5 at n = 1,000.

Golden set from real logs and failures, versioned, with a held-out split. Error analysis: read, note, categorize, count, fix the biggest.

🧰 pytest + JSONL, promptfoo, Braintrust, LangSmith

📐

5.2 Metrics

pass@k = 1 − C(n−c, k) ÷ C(n, k).

Code checks and exact match wherever a rule exists. BLEU/ROUGE reward shared words, not truth; embeddings miss numbers and negation. Run code to grade code.

🧰 HF evaluate, sacrebleu, sentence-transformers, Pydantic, human-eval

⚖️

5.3 LLM-as-judge

κ = (po − pe) ÷ (1 − pe); swap order for pairwise.

Binary criteria with a critique first. Biases: position, verbosity, self-preference, maths. Calibrate on expert labels; pin the judge (drift).

🧰 DeepEval GEval, promptfoo llm-rubric, autoevals, scikit-learn

📚

5.4 RAG evals

Faithfulness = supported claims ÷ claims; recall@k first, then MRR/nDCG.

Score retrieval and generation separately, then end to end. RAG triad works without references. Faithful ≠ correct. Label docs or spans, not chunk IDs.

🧰 RAGAS, DeepEval, TruLens, Phoenix, ranx

🤖

5.5 Agent evals

pass^k = C(c, k) ÷ C(n, k): what repeat users feel.

Outcome in a sandbox first, then trajectory rules (required steps, forbidden actions), cost per success, repeated trials. Benchmarks shortlist; your tasks decide.

🧰 Inspect, agentevals, promptfoo trajectory asserts, SWE-bench, τ-bench

🚦

5.6 Eval ops

Paired SE = √((b + c) − (b − c)²/n) ÷ n.

Must-pass suite blocks; quality suite blocks on significant paired regressions, warns otherwise. Online: thumbs + implicit + outcomes + guardrails; watch drift.

🧰 promptfoo in GitHub Actions, Langfuse, Phoenix, LangSmith, Braintrust

🗂️

5.7 Eval datasets

Coverage first: stratify by topic, difficulty and risk.

Mix real logs, synthetic variations and adversarial cases; compare the set's mix with production's and refresh it from new traffic.

🧰 trace exports, RAGAS test-set generation, spreadsheets

🧑‍⚖️

5.8 Human evaluation

Guidelines with examples, then measure inter-annotator agreement.

Humans are the reference judges calibrate against: use domain experts where stakes are high, and resolve disagreements into clearer rules.

🧰 annotation queues in Langfuse/LangSmith, Label Studio, κ

🧩

5.9 Evals by task

Match the grader to the task.

Classification → precision/recall; extraction → per-field exact match; summarization → faithfulness + coverage; code → tests; chat → rubric judges.

🧰 the metrics of 5.2, judges of 5.3

🛡️

5.10 Safety evals

Adversarial cases live in a must-pass suite.

Prompt injection, jailbreaks, PII leaks, harmful content, and robustness to paraphrases and typos. The attacker's view is Phase R.

🧰 promptfoo red-team, Inspect, guardrail metrics

🏁

5.11 Comparing models

Same cases, paired statistics, confidence intervals.

Public benchmarks and arenas shortlist models; your eval set, with cost and latency, decides.

🧰 leaderboards, your golden set, paired tests

🗣️ Teach it back

Answer out loud, like in an interview: “Our RAG-based support agent, which can issue refunds, launches next month. How will you know it's good enough to launch, and how will you know if it gets worse afterwards?”

A strong answer mentions: success criteria per task; a golden set from real tickets and failures with error bars; code checks for hard rules; a calibrated judge with κ; retrieval metrics (recall@k) separate from faithfulness and answer relevance; sandboxed agent tasks graded on final state, policy rules and pass^k; a CI gate with a must-pass suite and paired comparisons; an A/B or canary with guardrail metrics; online judges, feedback and drift alerts; and the loop from production failures back into the dataset.

🏗️ Build it yourself: the eval plan for a refund-capable support agent

✏️ The problem

Acme Gear's support assistant answers policy questions from a 300-page handbook (RAG) and can call tools: lookup_order, check_policy, issue_refund (up to $200 without a human), escalate_to_human. About 20,000 conversations a week. The team changes prompts several times a week and may switch model providers. Design the evaluation system: datasets, graders, CI gate, launch, production monitoring, and a budget.

Sketch it with numbers first. Then open the reveal.

Reveal: one good answer

Success criteria

  • Policy answers: correct and faithful to the handbook; abstain when the handbook doesn't say.
  • Refunds: right order, right amount, exactly once, only after the policy check; never above $200 without escalation.
  • Safety: no PII of other customers, resists prompt injection in user messages and order notes.
  • Experience: resolves without a human where it should; escalates angry or legal cases.

Datasets (versioned in git)

  • Policy Q&A: 400 questions sampled from real tickets (PII scrubbed), stratified by topic, each with the source passage labelled by document and span, plus 40 unanswerable ones.
  • Agent tasks: 80 scenarios in a sandbox (seeded orders DB, mock payment API, LLM-simulated customer), each with a goal state and rules.
  • Must-pass: 60 safety and critical-fact cases (injections, PII probes, refund limits, the Plus 60-day rule).
  • Held-out 20% of each, untouched during prompt tuning; 30 new cases a month from production failures.

Graders

  • Retrieval: recall@5 and MRR from labels (free, seconds).
  • Generation: claim-level faithfulness and answer relevance with a judge from another model family, binary per criterion, calibrated on 150 expert-labelled answers until κ > 0.7.
  • Agents: final-state checks, trajectory rules, tool-argument exact match, 5 trials per task → pass^5, cost per successful task.
  • Code assertions for format, refund limits and PII patterns.

Numbers

  • 400 Q&A at ~85% pass: ±3.5 pts per score, so gate on paired differences, which detect ~2–3 pt regressions.
  • CI cost per prompt PR: 400 answers + 400 judge calls + 80 tasks × 5 trials × ~$0.30 ≈ $3 + $3 + $120. So agent tasks run nightly and on model or tool changes; a 20-task smoke subset runs per PR (~$30). Prices illustrative.
  • Online: judge 2% of traffic (400 conversations a week) for faithfulness and policy.
  • A/B on resolution rate: a 2-point lift on a ~60% base needs ~9,000 conversations per arm: about a week at 50/50.

Gate and launch

  1. Must-pass: any failure blocks.
  2. Quality: block on a significant paired regression larger than 1 pt; warn and require review otherwise. Cost and latency budgets per case.
  3. Model switches: full suite + agent suite with pass^k, then a 5% canary with guardrails (escalations, refund volume, complaints) before 50/50.

Production

  • Tracing on every conversation; thumbs, rephrase and escalation signals; refund amount and count anomalies; “I don't know” rate; answer length; pinned model and judge versions.
  • Weekly error analysis of 50 thumbs-down and escalated conversations with a support lead; new failure categories become cases.

Classic mistakes

  • Grading agents by their final message instead of the refunds table.
  • One end-to-end score, so nobody knows whether retrieval or generation broke.
  • A judge nobody calibrated, or an unpinned judge that drifts.
  • Optimizing thumbs-up alone (the sycophancy trap).
  • Labels keyed to chunk IDs, broken by the next re-chunk.
  • Running eval agents against the real payment API.

🏆 Boss quiz

1. Your eval has 400 cases and the bot passes 75%. What's the 95% error bar, roughly?
SE = √(0.75 × 0.25 ÷ 400) = 0.0217; × 1.96 ≈ 4.2 points (Lesson 5.1).
2. An agent task was run 10 times and succeeded 7 times. What's its pass^3?
pass^3 = C(7, 3) ÷ C(10, 3) = 35 ÷ 120 = 0.29. 0.7³ = 0.34 is the with-replacement approximation; 0.99 is close to pass@3 (Lesson 5.5).
3. Judge and expert agree on 90% of labels; each passes 80% of answers. What's Cohen's κ?
pe = 0.8 × 0.8 + 0.2 × 0.2 = 0.68. κ = (0.90 − 0.68) ÷ (1 − 0.68) = 0.69: substantial (Lesson 5.3).
4. Your RAG eval shows context recall 0.95 but faithfulness 0.60. What do you fix first?
The right context is there (recall 0.95), but answers add claims it doesn't support. That's the generator's half of the pipeline (Lesson 5.4).
5. A new model is 2 points higher on a public leaderboard and 1 point higher on your 200-case eval, measured as two separate scores. Switch?
1 point on 200 unpaired cases is well inside the noise (±6 points or so). A paired comparison on your own cases, plus the cost, reliability and safety checks, is what decides (Lessons 5.1, 5.6, 5.11).
