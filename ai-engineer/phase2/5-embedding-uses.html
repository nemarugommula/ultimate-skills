Phase 2 · Lesson 5

Beyond search: classification, clustering, dedup, recommendations

Once text is a vector, “nearby” answers far more than search queries: which category is this, what topics exist, which items are duplicates, what else will this user like, and what looks unusual.

🧰 You'll need (quick reminders)
  • Embeddings map similar meanings to nearby vectors; cosine similarity measures how near (Lesson 2.1).
  • ANN indexes find the k nearest vectors fast (2.2); k-means groups points around centres (used by IVF in 2.2).
  • Cost per million tokens for embeddings vs LLM calls (Lessons 0.3 and 2.1).
🎯 Hook

Web-scale training sets are full of near-copies: the same photo with a different caption, the same article on ten sites. Meta researchers embedded a subset of LAION (a big image-text dataset) and removed pairs whose embeddings were nearly identical. Their method, SemDeDup, threw away 50% of the data with minimal performance loss, effectively halving training time, and performance outside the training distribution even went up.

No new model, no labels: just “these two vectors are too close, keep one”. That's an embedding doing a job other than search.

Numbers first: the same vectors, five jobs

JobWhat you computeTypical recipe
ClassificationWhich labelled examples or label descriptions are nearest?k-nearest neighbours, nearest centroid, or a small classifier (logistic regression) on top of embeddings
ClusteringWhich items group together, with no labels?k-means or HDBSCAN on embeddings, then an LLM names each cluster
DeduplicationWhich pairs are closer than a threshold?ANN top-k neighbours per item, cosine ≥ threshold, plus rules (IDs must match)
RecommendationsWhich items are near what this user liked?Item-to-item neighbours; user vector = average of liked items
Anomaly / drift detectionWhich items are far from everything else?Distance to the k-th nearest neighbour or to cluster centres, tracked over time
✏️ Worked example: classify a million tickets a day

1,000,000 support tickets a day, about 30 tokens each.

  • Embeddings + nearest centroid: 30 million tokens × $0.02 per million (text-embedding-3-small) = $0.60 a day, plus one dot product per label (microseconds).
  • An LLM call per ticket with a 300-token instruction prompt: about 330 million input tokens. At an illustrative $0.25 per million input tokens for a small model, that's about $83 a day, and each call takes hundreds of milliseconds.

Roughly 100× cheaper. The LLM is more flexible (it can explain itself, handle new labels from instructions alone) and often more accurate on subtle cases; the usual design is embeddings for the bulk, LLM for the uncertain few. LLM prices are illustrative; check current pricing.

💡 Big idea Every task here is the same question in disguise: how close are these vectors, and where do I draw the line? The embedding gives you distances for free; the engineering is choosing the threshold, the neighbours or the centres, and checking the mistakes on both sides of the line.
🧠 Analogy: a party seen from above

Look down at a party where people stand near people they have things in common with. Clustering: notice the groups (the runners by the kitchen, the musicians by the stereo). Classification: a newcomer walks in; whichever group they drift to tells you who they are. Dedup: two people standing on the same floor tile are probably twins. Recommendation: introduce someone to the people next to their friends. Anomaly: the person alone in the corner is the one to check on.

Classification without training a model

  • Zero-shot (label descriptions): embed a sentence describing each label (“Problems signing in: password, login, locked out”) and pick the nearest. No examples needed; works when labels are about clearly different topics.
  • Few-shot (nearest centroid): embed a handful of real examples per label, average them into a centroid, pick the nearest centroid. A few real examples usually beat a clever description, because they look like real traffic.
  • k-NN: find the k most similar labelled examples and take a majority vote; great when you have thousands of labelled items and labels change often (just add examples).
  • Train a small classifier (logistic regression) on embeddings: with a few hundred labels per class it's usually the most accurate cheap option, and still trains in seconds on a laptop.

In every case, add an “unsure” zone: if the top score is low or the top two labels are close, send the item to an LLM or a human.

Clustering: discover the topics you didn't know about

Embed 50,000 support tickets, run k-means (pick k centres, assign each point to the nearest, move centres to the mean, repeat) or a density method like HDBSCAN (finds clusters of any shape and leaves outliers unassigned), then ask an LLM to name each cluster from a few samples. You get a map of what users actually talk about: “refund for duplicate charge”, “export to CSV broken”, “can't add team member”. BERTopic packages this recipe (embeddings → dimensionality reduction → clustering → keyword labels). Anthropic's Clio applies it to conversations, with privacy rules on top. How to pick k and check clusters is in the Math for AI course's k-means lesson.

Deduplication: one threshold, two kinds of mistake

Compare every pair and you're dead: 1 million documents have about 500 billion pairs. Instead, use an ANN index to fetch each document's 10 nearest neighbours (10 million checks), and merge pairs above a cosine threshold. The threshold is a dial between:

  • False merges (threshold too low): different things treated as one. A refund for order 1182 merged with a refund for order 2290 loses a customer's request.
  • Missed duplicates (threshold too high): the same thing counted twice. Your “top issues” dashboard double counts, and your RAG index returns three copies of one paragraph.

Fixes beyond the threshold: add guard rules (IDs, numbers, dates must match), a review band just below the threshold that goes to a human or an LLM, and exact hashing first for byte-identical copies (cheaper than embeddings).

Recommendations, anomalies and caching

  • Recommendations. “More like this” is a nearest-neighbour query on the item's vector. For a user, average the vectors of what they liked recently and search near that. At scale, recommendation systems learn the vectors from behaviour (clicks, plays, graph structure) rather than text, but the serving step is the same ANN lookup: Pinterest's PinSage and Spotify's Voyager (Lesson 2.2) are exactly this.
  • Anomaly and drift detection. If a new ticket's nearest neighbour is far away, it's something new: a fresh bug, a new fraud pattern, an abuse attempt. Track the average distance of today's queries to your corpus: if it creeps up, users are asking about things your documents don't cover (a RAG coverage gap, Lesson 5.4).
  • Semantic caching (Lesson 8.2). Reuse an earlier LLM answer when a new question's embedding is close enough to a cached one. Same threshold problem as dedup: in our toy model “How do I cancel my order?” and “Cancel my subscription” score 0.77, so a loose threshold would answer one with the other.
🧰 Tools you'll actually use

scikit-learn (KMeans, LogisticRegression, NearestNeighbors, HDBSCAN), sentence-transformers utilities (paraphrase_mining, community_detection), BERTopic for topic discovery, Faiss or your vector database for neighbours at scale, and GPTCache or gateway features for semantic caching.

import numpy as np
from sentence_transformers import SentenceTransformer
from sentence_transformers.util.retrieval import paraphrase_mining
from sklearn.cluster import KMeans

model = SentenceTransformer("Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B")

# 1. Few-shot classification by nearest centroid
examples = {"billing": ["I was billed twice", "Send me a receipt"],
            "access":  ["Can't log in", "Reset my password"]}
labels = list(examples)
centroids = np.stack([model.encode(examples[l], normalize_embeddings=True).mean(0) for l in labels])
centroids /= np.linalg.norm(centroids, axis=1, keepdims=True)
v = model.encode(["my 2FA code never arrives"], normalize_embeddings=True)[0]
scores = centroids @ v
best, second = np.sort(scores)[::-1][:2]
label = labels[int(scores.argmax())] if best - second > 0.05 else "unsure → LLM"

# 2. Near-duplicate pairs: [score, i, j], most similar first
pairs = paraphrase_mining(model, tickets)
dups = [(i, j) for s, i, j in pairs if s >= 0.9]           # pick the threshold on a labelled sample

# 3. Topic discovery
X = model.encode(tickets, normalize_embeddings=True)
topics = KMeans(n_clusters=30, n_init="auto").fit_predict(X)   # then ask an LLM to name each cluster

🚀 Startup vs big tech

Week one

Reuse the embeddings you already compute for RAG: nearest-centroid routing of tickets, a weekly k-means of user questions named by an LLM, and dedup of your knowledge base before indexing. All of it is a few dozen lines with scikit-learn.

At scale

Embedding-based classifiers as a cheap first stage with LLM escalation, dedup pipelines over billions of training examples (SemDeDup-style), recommendation vectors learned from behaviour and served from ANN indexes, and embedding-drift dashboards that alert when traffic shifts.

🎤 Interview angle

“We get 2 million support tickets a month. How would you route them to the right team?” A senior answer:

  • Starts with cost and latency math: an LLM per ticket vs embeddings plus a classifier.
  • Proposes embeddings + small classifier or nearest centroid on historical labelled tickets, with an uncertainty band escalated to an LLM or a human.
  • Mentions clustering unlabelled tickets to find new categories, and dedup to group incident storms (“500 tickets, one outage”).
  • Measures with per-class precision and recall on a held-out set, and monitors drift as products change.
🏭 In the real world
Semantic dedup of training data 🧪 Research · 2023 SemDeDup (Abbas et al., Meta)

How: embed every example with a pre-trained model, cluster, and within each cluster remove pairs above a similarity threshold.

Why this method: exact-match dedup misses near-copies; removing semantic duplicates cut a LAION subset by 50% with minimal loss and halved training time.

What are people using Claude for? 🏢 Anthropic · 2024 Clio: privacy-preserving insights

How: an LLM summarizes each conversation into facets (topic, intent), the summaries are embedded and grouped with k-means (often thousands of clusters), and clusters are arranged into a hierarchy and named.

Why this method: nobody reads raw conversations; clustering on summaries surfaces usage patterns and coordinated abuse, and a minimum number of unique users per cluster protects privacy.

Embeddings for web-scale recommendations 🏢 Pinterest · 2018 PinSage

How: a graph neural network produces embeddings for pins from both their content and the pin–board graph (3 billion nodes, 18 billion edges); recommendations are nearest neighbours.

Why this method: combining what an item looks like with how people use it beat comparable deep-learning and graph-based alternatives in offline metrics, user studies and A/B tests.

Topics from embeddings 🧰 Open source BERTopic

How: embed documents, reduce dimensions, cluster (HDBSCAN by default), then describe each cluster with its most distinctive words (class-based TF-IDF).

Why this method: it turns “we have 100,000 unlabelled texts” into a browsable list of topics in minutes, and you can swap any embedding model or clustering algorithm in.

🤯 Fun fact · zero-shot by description, for images

OpenAI's CLIP (2021) embeds images and text in one shared space. To classify an image, it embeds captions like “a photo of a dog” for each class and picks the nearest, exactly the label-description trick from the playground. With no ImageNet training examples at all, it matched the accuracy of the original ResNet-50, which had been trained on the 1.28 million labelled images.

Check yourself

1. You want near-duplicates among 2 million documents. Comparing every pair vs. checking each document's 20 nearest neighbours from an ANN index: roughly how many similarity checks each?
All pairs: 2M × (2M − 1) ÷ 2 ≈ 2 × 10¹². Neighbours: 2M × 20 = 40 million, 50,000× fewer. That's why dedup at scale always goes through an ANN index (or MinHash for exact-ish text copies).
2. Your dedup merged “Refund order 1182” with “Refund order 2290” (cosine 0.84). Raising the threshold to 0.86 fixes it but now misses real duplicates. Best fix?
Embeddings blur numbers (Lesson 2.1), so no threshold separates “same text, different order number” cleanly. A cheap structured check on the fields that must match solves exactly that error class without hurting the paraphrase duplicates.
3. A zero-shot classifier using label descriptions misroutes 15% of tickets. You have 3,000 historical tickets with team labels. What's the strongest next step?
Real labelled examples capture how customers actually write, which descriptions can't. A logistic regression on embeddings trains in seconds and usually beats zero-shot clearly; the uncertainty band sends the hard few to an LLM or a person.
📝 TL;DR
  • Embeddings power classification, clustering, dedup, recommendations and anomaly detection, often ~100× cheaper than an LLM per item.
  • Classification: label descriptions (zero-shot) → a few examples (centroid) → a small classifier; always keep an “unsure” band.
  • Clustering + an LLM to name clusters reveals the topics you didn't know about.
  • Dedup is a threshold with two error types; add guard rules and review bands, and use ANN, never all pairs.
  • Every threshold depends on the model: choose it on a labelled sample.
