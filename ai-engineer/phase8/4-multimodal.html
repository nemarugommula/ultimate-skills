Phase 8 · Lesson 4

Multimodal AI: vision, speech, image generation & realtime voice

Models now read screenshots, transcribe calls, talk back in real time and draw pictures. Under the hood it's the same idea as text: everything becomes tokens. Which means everything has a token bill and a latency budget.

🧰 You'll need (quick reminders)
  • Models read and write tokens, and you pay per token (Lesson 0.3).
  • TTFT (time to first token) and tokens per second decide how fast an answer appears (Lesson 0.5); streaming shows it as it's generated.
  • Lesson 4.5 used images inside RAG (visual documents); this lesson covers the modalities themselves.
🎯 Hook

Before May 2024, ChatGPT's Voice Mode chained three models: one turned speech into text, a text model answered, a third turned the answer back into speech. OpenAI reported average response times of 2.8 seconds with GPT-3.5 and 5.4 seconds with GPT-4. Talking to it felt like a walkie-talkie.

GPT-4o was trained end to end on text, audio and images, so one model hears you and speaks back. It responds to audio in as little as 232 ms, 320 ms on average. For comparison, a 10-language study of human conversation found the typical gap between one person finishing and the next starting is about 200 ms.

A screenshot costs about as much as a page of text. An hour of audio costs about as much as a novel. Those two facts drive most multimodal design decisions.

💡 Big idea A multimodal model turns pixels and sound into the same kind of tokens as text (an encoder chops the input into patches or short frames and maps each to a vector), then reasons over all of them together. So the engineering questions are the familiar ones: how many tokens does this input become, how fast does the answer come back, and what does it get wrong.
🧠 Analogy: a translator with a mosaic

The model only speaks “token”. An image gets cut into a mosaic of little tiles, and each tile is described as one word in that language. A bigger photo means more tiles: more words to read, more cost, more detail. Shrink the photo and the tiles get coarse: cheap, but the fine print on a receipt becomes unreadable.

Vision: what models see

A vision-language model (VLM) pairs an image encoder, often a vision transformer that splits the image into patches, with a language model. That lets it describe photos, read text in images (OCR, optical character recognition), answer questions about charts and screenshots, and extract fields from forms. Typical weak spots: tiny text after downscaling, exact counting, precise positions and measurements, and dense tables. Two practical rules: send the resolution the task needs (crop to the region that matters rather than sending a huge photo), and for documents, test page images vs parsed text on your own data (Lesson 4.5).

✏️ Worked example: digitising 10,000 scanned contract pages

Each page scanned at 200 dpi is 1654×2339 px. At an illustrative $3 per million input tokens:

  • Claude standard tier: downscaled to about 924×1307 → ⌈924/28⌉ × ⌈1307/28⌉ = 33 × 47 = 1,551 tokens → 10,000 pages ≈ 15.5 M tokens ≈ $47.
  • OpenAI tiles (GPT-4o rule): shortest side to 768 → 768×1086 → 2 × 3 = 6 tiles → 85 + 6 × 170 = 1,105 tokens → ≈ $33.
  • Claude high-res tier: 1624×2296 → 4,756 tokens → ≈ $143, but 8-point footnotes are still legible.

The image tokens are cheap either way. What matters is whether the downscaled page is still readable; test accuracy on a sample at each setting before picking the cheap one. Output tokens (the extracted text, say 600 tokens a page at a higher output price) can easily cost more than the image.

🎮 Playground · Voice-agent latency budget

Time from the moment the user stops talking until they hear the first sound of the reply. Start with the 2023-style pipeline, then stream it, then compare a speech-to-speech model. Then try to get a cascade under one second.

A budget calculator with typical, assumed numbers (a 70-token answer whose first sentence is 15 tokens; batch STT ≈ 600 ms for a 3-second utterance). Measure every stage in your own traces; vendors' latency claims usually exclude your network and application.

What you should see: without streaming, the user waits for the whole answer to be generated and synthesised; streaming means you only wait for the first sentence. After that the biggest piece is usually the end-of-turn wait, which is why turn detection gets so much attention, and why making it too eager backfires. A speech-to-speech model removes two hops and the text round trip.

✏️ Worked example: the streamed cascade budget

End-of-turn wait 500 ms + streaming STT final transcript 150 ms + network 60 ms + LLM time to first token 450 ms + first sentence (15 tokens at 80 tokens/s ≈ 190 ms) + TTS first audio 120 ms + network 60 ms ≈ 1.5 s. Without streaming the same stages take about 3.8 s, because the 70-token answer (≈ 875 ms) and about 21 seconds of speech must be fully generated and synthesised before anything plays.

Image generation: diffusion in one paragraph

Diffusion models learn to remove noise. In training, you add noise to real images step by step and teach a network to predict and remove it (DDPM, Ho et al., 2020). To generate, start from pure noise and denoise it many times, steering each step toward the text prompt. Latent diffusion (Rombach et al., the basis of Stable Diffusion) does this in a small compressed “latent” space instead of on pixels, which made it far cheaper to train and run. Today's hosted options include OpenAI's gpt-image models and Google's Gemini image models (nicknamed “Nano Banana”); names and prices change often, so check the docs. Engineering concerns: cost per image by size and quality, latency (seconds, not milliseconds), content policy and moderation, and labelling AI-generated images (Lesson 8.5).

🚀 Startup vs big tech

Week one

Use a hosted multimodal API for everything: vision for document extraction, a realtime speech-to-speech API or a voice framework for agents. Log transcripts and image token counts from day one, resize images before upload, and keep a 50-example eval set per modality.

At scale

Per-stage vendors chosen by measured latency and accuracy, self-hosted Whisper-class models for bulk transcription, image preprocessing (crop, deskew, tile) tuned per document type, a telephony stack with regional servers to cut network hops, and dashboards for time-to-first-audio and interruption rates.

🎤 Interview angle

“Design a voice agent for a pharmacy's phone line.” A senior answer:

  • Sets a latency budget (first audio within about a second) and breaks it down by stage, including the phone network.
  • Chooses cascade vs speech-to-speech on purpose: transcripts for compliance and guardrails vs naturalness and speed.
  • Covers turn detection and barge-in, streaming at every stage, and filler (“let me check that”) during slow tool calls.
  • Handles accuracy where it matters: confirm drug names and dates back to the caller, hand off to a human when unsure.
  • Estimates cost per minute (STT + LLM tokens + TTS, or audio tokens) and measures word error rate on real calls.
🏭 In the real world
Seeing for people who are blind 🏢 Be My Eyes · 2023 Be My Eyes and GPT-4

How: users photograph something (a label, a menu, a street) and a GPT-4-powered “Virtual Volunteer”, later called Be My AI, describes it and answers follow-up questions.

Why this method: image understanding plus conversation replaces a wait for a human volunteer for many everyday tasks; human volunteers stay available.

One model for text, audio and vision 🏢 OpenAI · 2024 Hello GPT-4o

How: a single network trained end to end across modalities replaced the three-model voice pipeline.

Why this method: response time dropped from 2.8–5.4 s on average to 320 ms, and the model can hear tone and multiple speakers, which a transcript throws away.

Speech recognition from weak supervision 🧪 Research · 2022 Whisper (Radford et al.)

How: a transformer trained on 680,000 hours of multilingual, multitask audio-transcript pairs from the web.

Why this method: training on a huge, weakly labelled web dataset made it robust to accents, noise and new domains without fine-tuning; the open weights became the default self-hosted STT.

Diffusion in a compressed space 🧪 Research · 2021 High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models

How: an autoencoder compresses images to a small latent; the diffusion model denoises there, guided by text through cross-attention.

Why this method: far less compute than pixel-space diffusion, which made open text-to-image models like Stable Diffusion practical.

🤯 Fun fact · 77 years of listening

Whisper's 680,000 hours of training audio is about 77.6 years of non-stop sound (680,000 ÷ 24 ÷ 365). A person listening eight hours a day would need over 230 years to get through it.

Check yourself

1. Claude counts one token per 28×28 patch. You send 5,000 product photos at 1000×1000 px, at $3 per million input tokens. What do the images cost?
⌈1000/28⌉ = 36, so 36 × 36 = 1,296 tokens per image; × 5,000 = 6.48 M tokens; × $3/M ≈ $19.44. Cropping to the product or resizing to 500×500 (324 tokens) would cut that by about 4×.
2. Your voice agent takes 3 seconds to start replying. It waits for the full LLM answer, then synthesises it all. What's the biggest single fix?
Without streaming you wait for the whole answer plus the whole synthesis. Streaming makes you wait only for the first sentence. A 50 ms end-of-turn wait would interrupt users constantly.
3. A bank's phone agent must log exactly what was said, block unsafe answers before they're spoken, and use the bank's own fine-tuned text model. Which architecture fits best?
The cascade gives a transcript to log, a text answer to check before it's spoken, and a free choice of LLM. It costs some latency; streaming every stage keeps that to around a second or so.
📝 TL;DR
  • Images, audio and video become tokens: know your provider's rule (patches, tiles, tokens per second) and send only the resolution the task needs.
  • Vision models read screenshots, charts and documents well, but struggle with tiny text after downscaling, counting and precise positions: test on your data.
  • Voice: a cascade (STT → LLM → TTS) gives control; speech-to-speech gives speed. Stream every stage; turn detection is the hidden latency.
  • Diffusion generates images by denoising; latent diffusion made it cheap. Hosted image APIs change fast; check names, prices and policies.
