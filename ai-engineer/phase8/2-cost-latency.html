Phase 8 · Lesson 2

Cutting cost & latency: caching, routing, batching

Most LLM bills are full of tokens nobody needed to pay for: the same system prompt sent a million times, a frontier model answering “what are your opening hours?”, a nightly job paying interactive prices. Here are the levers, what each saves, and what each can break.

🧰 You'll need (quick reminders)
  • You pay per input and output token; output tokens usually cost several times more each (Lesson 0.3).
  • TTFT (time to first token) is how long until the first word appears; streaming shows words as they're generated (Lesson 0.5).
  • Embeddings map text to vectors; cosine similarity near 1 means “similar meaning” (Lesson 2.1). Lesson 8.1 showed where a request's cost comes from.
🎯 Hook

In 2023, Stanford researchers asked a simple question: do you really need the most expensive model for every query? Their system, FrugalGPT, sends each query to a cheap model first and only escalates to a pricier one when a small scorer thinks the cheap answer isn't good enough.

The result: it could match the best single model they tested (GPT-4) with up to 98% lower cost, or beat its accuracy by 4% at the same cost. Most queries are easy. The trick is spending big only on the hard ones.

Numbers first: where the money goes

A support assistant: 2 million requests a month. Each sends a 3,000-token fixed prefix (system prompt, tool definitions, examples), 2,000 tokens of retrieved chunks and history, and gets 350 tokens back. Illustrative prices: $3 / $15 per million input/output tokens for a large model.

The biggest line is text that is identical on every request. That's the first thing to stop paying full price for.

💡 Big idea Every cost lever is one of four moves: don't recompute (prompt caching, semantic caching), use a smaller model when it's enough (routing, cascades, distillation), send and generate fewer tokens (trim context, shorter outputs), or pay less per token by waiting (batch APIs). Latency mostly follows cost: fewer tokens and smaller models are faster too. Each move has a quality risk you must measure.
🧠 Analogy: running a restaurant kitchen

Prompt caching is mise en place: chop the onions once in the morning, not per order. A semantic cache is the counter of ready-made sandwiches: instant, but if someone orders “no mustard” and you hand them the standard one, that's a wrong order. Routing is letting the junior cook make the salads and saving the head chef for the soufflé. Batching is catering: order a day ahead and it's half price.

Prompt caching: stop paying for the same prefix

When a model reads a prompt, it computes internal state (the KV cache, Lesson 7.4) for every token. If the next request starts with the same tokens, the provider can reuse that state instead of recomputing it. Two things follow: put the stable stuff first (system prompt, tools, examples, documents) and the variable stuff (the user's question) last, and keep the stable part byte-for-byte identical (no timestamps at the top).

  • Anthropic (as of 2026): you mark cache breakpoints with cache_control. Reading cached tokens costs 0.1× the base input price on most models; writing them costs 1.25× for a 5-minute lifetime or 2× for 1 hour. The minimum cacheable prompt length depends on the model (512 to 4,096 tokens). At launch in 2024 Anthropic reported up to 90% lower cost and up to 85% lower latency for long prompts.
  • OpenAI: caching is automatic for prompts of 1,024 tokens or more. The cached-token discount differs by model, and the newest models also bill cache writes, so check the pricing page for yours.
✏️ Worked example: caching the 3,000-token prefix

1 million requests a month, a 3,000-token prefix, $3 per million input tokens, and suppose 90% of requests hit the cache (traffic is steady enough that the cache rarely expires).

  • No caching: 3,000 × 1,000,000 × $3 / 1,000,000 = $9,000.
  • Hits pay 0.1×, misses pay the 1.25× write: 0.9 × 0.1 + 0.1 × 1.25 = 0.09 + 0.125 = 0.215 of full price.
  • With caching: $9,000 × 0.215 = $1,935. A 78% saving on that line, for moving the question to the end of the prompt.

If traffic is sparse and the cache expires between requests, every request pays the 1.25× write: caching then costs you 25% more. Watch the cached-token counts the API returns.

Semantic caching: the threshold is everything

A semantic cache embeds each incoming question and finds the most similar past question. If the similarity is above a threshold, it returns the stored answer, skipping the model completely. A low threshold gives many hits and some confidently wrong answers; a high one is safe but rarely hits.

Similarity uses this course's tiny offline embedding. Real embedding models score differently, so real thresholds differ (RedisVL's default, for example, is a cosine distance of 0.1, i.e. similarity ≈ 0.9), but the trade-off has the same shape.

What you should see: there is no threshold that catches every rephrasing without serving a wrong answer. “How much does express shipping cost?” stays very similar to “How much does shipping cost?” at any setting, yet the answer differs. Real systems add guards: only cache answers that don't depend on the user (no order status, no account data), include key entities (plan, product, locale) in the cache key, expire entries, and measure the wrong-answer rate on a labelled sample.

🧰 Tools you'll actually use

Provider features first: prompt caching and batch APIs (Anthropic Message Batches, OpenAI Batch API). For semantic caching: RedisVL SemanticCache, GPTCache, or a cache in your gateway (LiteLLM, Cloudflare AI Gateway). For routing: your gateway's routing rules, or a trained router such as RouteLLM.

import anthropic
client = anthropic.Anthropic()

# 1) Prompt caching: stable content first, marked with a cache breakpoint
resp = client.messages.create(
    model=MODEL, max_tokens=400,
    system=[{"type": "text", "text": LONG_SYSTEM_PROMPT_AND_POLICIES,
             "cache_control": {"type": "ephemeral"}}],   # 5-minute cache by default
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": question}],             # the variable part goes last
)
print(resp.usage.cache_read_input_tokens, resp.usage.cache_creation_input_tokens)

# 2) Batch API: half price for work nobody is waiting on
batch = client.messages.batches.create(requests=[
    {"custom_id": f"doc-{i}",
     "params": {"model": MODEL, "max_tokens": 300,

    for i, d in enumerate(docs)])

# 3) Semantic cache with RedisVL (distance, not similarity: lower = stricter)
from redisvl.extensions.cache.llm import SemanticCache
cache = SemanticCache(name="faq", redis_url="redis://localhost:6379", distance_threshold=0.1)
if hit := cache.check(prompt=question):

🚀 Startup vs big tech

Week one

Order prompts stable-first and turn on prompt caching; stream every user-facing answer; cap max_tokens; move anything nightly to a batch API. Don't build a router until traces show which traffic is expensive and evals show a cheaper model is good enough on it.

At scale

A cost dashboard per feature, routers trained on real traffic and checked against evals, cascades with a confidence check, distilled task-specific models for the highest-volume paths, semantic caches scoped per tenant, and capacity reservations negotiated with providers.

🎤 Interview angle

“Our LLM bill is $200k a month. Cut it in half without hurting quality.” A senior answer:

  • Starts with measurement: cost per feature and per step from traces; where are the tokens (prefix, context, output)?
  • Takes the safe wins first: prompt caching with a stable prefix, trimming context, shorter outputs, batch for async work.
  • Then routing or a cascade on the high-volume, easy traffic, gated by an offline eval and an online A/B test.
  • Semantic caching only where answers don't depend on the user, with a measured wrong-answer rate.
  • Names the guardrail: a quality metric that must not drop, checked continuously (Lesson 5.6).
🏭 In the real world
Cheap model first, escalate when unsure 🧪 Research · 2023 FrugalGPT (Chen, Zaharia, Zou)

How: an LLM cascade: query models in order of price, score each answer with a small model, stop when the score is high enough.

Why this method: matched GPT-4's performance with up to 98% cost reduction, or improved accuracy by 4% at the same cost, across their benchmarks.

Learned routers between a strong and a weak model 🧪 Research · 2024 RouteLLM

How: train a router on human preference data to predict when the weak model's answer will be as good as the strong one's.

Why this method: cut costs by over 2× in some cases without hurting response quality, and routers transferred to model pairs they weren't trained on.

Prompt caching 🏢 Anthropic · 2024 Prompt caching docs

How: mark a prefix with cache_control; later requests with the same prefix read it at a fraction of the input price.

Why this method: long, repeated prompts (big documents, many examples, tool definitions) are where most input spend goes; the launch reported up to 90% lower cost and 85% lower latency for long prompts.

Half price for waiting 🏢 OpenAI Batch API guide

How: upload a file of requests, get results within a 24-hour window.

Why this method: 50% lower cost (and separate rate limits) for evals, backfills, classification and summarization jobs.

A semantic cache library 🧰 Open source GPTCache (Zilliz)

How: embeds queries, stores answers in a vector store, and returns a cached answer above a similarity threshold, with pluggable similarity checks.

Why this method: FAQ-style traffic repeats a lot; a cache answers it in milliseconds without a model call.

🤯 Fun fact · you may already be saving

OpenAI's prompt caching is on by default for prompts of 1,024 tokens or more, so many apps get the discount without changing a line, as long as their prompts start with the same tokens. Put a timestamp or the user's name at the very top and you quietly turn it off.

Check yourself

1. A 10,000-token prefix, 500,000 requests a month, $3 per million input tokens. With 80% cache hits at 0.1× and misses paying a 1.25× write, what does the prefix cost per month?
Full price: 10,000 × 500,000 × $3/M = $15,000. Multiplier: 0.8 × 0.1 + 0.2 × 1.25 = 0.08 + 0.25 = 0.33, so $4,950. $1,500 forgets the misses' write premium; $15,000 is no caching at all.
2. After adding a semantic cache, customers report being told the wrong refund window: Plus members get the regular-customer answer. What's the best fix?
“Refund as a Plus member” and “refund for an order” are similar texts with different answers. Putting plan type in the cache key stops cross-plan reuse; a stricter threshold and a measured wrong-answer rate keep it honest. Prompt caching never changes answers.
3. 70% of your traffic is simple FAQ questions. A small model is 15× cheaper and as good on those, but much worse on the rest. What do you ship?
Switching everything breaks the 30% hard queries; keeping the large model wastes most of the bill. Routing captures most of the saving; a cascade's confidence check catches the router's mistakes. Evals prove the quality held.
📝 TL;DR
  • Measure first: which feature and which part of the prompt costs the most.
  • Safe wins: stable-first prompts with prompt caching, trimmed context, shorter outputs, batch APIs (50%) for async work, streaming for perceived latency.
  • Routing and cascades save the most but risk quality: escalate when unsure and gate with evals.
  • Semantic caching trades hit rate for wrong answers at a threshold: scope it, measure it, and never share personal answers.
