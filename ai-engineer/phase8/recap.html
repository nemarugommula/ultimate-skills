Phase 8 · Recap

Recap: Production, safety & multimodal

A demo answers one question well. A product answers millions, through outages and attacks, within a budget, in text, images and voice, without treating anyone unfairly. This phase was about that gap.

🧾 Cheat sheet

🔭

8.1 LLMOps

Trace every step; quality fails silently. Spans with tokens, cost, latency, model and prompt version (OpenTelemetry gen_ai.*); tag by feature and user; gateway with short timeouts, few retries, fallbacks, circuit breaker; pin model snapshots. Langfuse, LangSmith, Phoenix, Helicone, LiteLLM, Portkey.

💸

8.2 Cost & latency

Cached prefix ≈ 0.1× price; batch = 50% off. Stable-first prompts, trim context, shorter outputs, stream for TTFT; route or cascade easy traffic to small models (FrugalGPT: up to 98% cheaper); semantic cache trades hits for wrong answers. Prompt caching, Batch APIs, RedisVL, RouteLLM.

🛡️

8.3 Guardrails

Private data + untrusted content + a way out = leak. Indirect injection is the core threat; filters lower the rate, architecture lowers the impact: least privilege, approval, output allow-lists, quarantine. Redact PII. OWASP LLM Top 10. Llama Guard 4, Prompt Guard 2, Presidio, moderation APIs.

🎙️

8.4 Multimodal

Everything becomes tokens. Claude: ⌈w/28⌉×⌈h/28⌉; OpenAI tiles: 85 + 170 per tile; send the resolution the task needs. Voice: stream every stage; end-of-turn wait is the hidden latency; cascade = control, speech-to-speech = speed. Realtime API, Gemini Live, Whisper, LiveKit, Pipecat.

⚖️

8.5 Responsible AI

Impact ratio < 0.8 = adverse impact; passing ≠ fair. Counterfactual audits with a paired test; minimise and expire data; disclose AI; humans own high-stakes calls; model cards; NIST AI RMF; EU AI Act tiers (high-risk from Dec 2027). Fairlearn, model cards, NIST AI RMF.

🗣️ Teach it back

An interviewer asks: “Your AI assistant is going from a pilot with 50 users to 2 million customers next quarter. What has to change, and what could go wrong?”

Say it out loud in two minutes. A strong answer covers: tracing and cost attribution, a gateway with timeouts and fallbacks, pinned versions and online evals, the cheapest safe cost levers (caching, batching, routing with evals), the trifecta review for every tool and data source, PII redaction and retention, and the legal and fairness checks for the markets and decisions involved.

🏗️ Build it yourself: a voice and chat agent for a bank's card line

A European bank wants an AI agent for its card-services line: lost or stolen cards, disputed transactions, “why was my card declined?”. It should work in the app's chat and on the phone. Expect 50,000 calls a day averaging 4 minutes, plus chat. It can look up transactions and freeze a card. Design it using this phase's ideas: operations, cost, security, the voice pipeline and responsible-AI obligations.

Reveal a strong answer

1. Requirements

  • First audio within about 1 s of the caller finishing; answers grounded in the bank's policies and the customer's own transactions only.
  • Freezing a card is allowed after identity verification and a spoken confirmation; refunds and disputes go to a human.
  • No customer data in logs beyond what's needed; full audit trail of every tool call.

2. Numbers

  • Concurrency: 50,000 × 4 min ÷ (12 busy hours × 60) ≈ 280 calls at once, plan for 2× at peak ≈ 560.
  • Per call, illustrative prices: STT 4 min × $0.0045 = $0.018; LLM 8 turns × (4,000 input + 150 output) at $3/$15 = $0.114, or about $0.049 with a cached 3,000-token prefix; TTS around $0.01. Total ≈ $0.08 a call ≈ $3,900 a day.
  • Latency budget (streamed cascade): end-of-turn 500 ms + STT 150 + LLM first token 450 + first sentence ~190 + TTS 120 + network ~120 ≈ 1.5 s; cut the end-of-turn wait with a turn-detection model and keep servers in-region.

3. Architecture (8.4)

  • A streamed cascade (STT → LLM → TTS), not speech-to-speech: the bank needs transcripts for compliance, a text answer to check before it's spoken, and its choice of model. Barge-in support and a filler line during slow lookups.
  • Chat uses the same agent and tools without the speech stages.

4. Operations (8.1)

  • OpenTelemetry traces per call: each turn, each tool, tokens, cost, time to first audio; tagged by channel and intent.
  • A gateway with 2–3 s timeouts on time to first token, one retry, a fallback model tested on the same eval set, and a circuit breaker; if every model is down, route to the human queue.
  • Pinned model snapshots and versioned prompts; online evals on sampled transcripts (groundedness, correct policy, handover rate).

5. Cost (8.2)

  • Policies and tool definitions first in the prompt, cached; trim history to the current call; concise spoken answers.
  • Route simple intents (“card declined at a shop?”) to a smaller model once evals show it's as good; nightly call summaries and quality scoring via a batch API.
  • No semantic cache for anything account-specific.

6. Security (8.3)

  • Trifecta review: private data (transactions), untrusted content (merchant names and descriptions in transactions, the caller themselves), ways out (tools, any links sent by SMS). Merchant text is treated as data; the agent can't send messages or fetch URLs at all.
  • Least privilege: get_transactions scoped to the verified customer; freeze_card needs verification plus confirmation; no unfreeze or payments by AI.
  • PII redaction before traces; card numbers never spoken back in full; retention limits on recordings and transcripts.

7. Responsible AI and regulation (8.5)

  • Tell callers at the start that they're speaking with an AI (EU AI Act transparency duties apply from August 2026) and offer a human at any time.
  • Customer support is not itself high-risk, but if the agent ever decided credit limits or loan eligibility it would be: keep those decisions out of scope.
  • Fairness checks: compare resolution and handover rates across languages and accents (word error rate by group), since speech recognition can serve some speakers worse.

8. Classic mistakes

  • No timeout: a hung model call means silence on the phone.
  • Letting the model read card numbers or merchant text into tool arguments without validation.
  • An over-eager end-of-turn timer that cuts off callers reading out long numbers.
  • Logging full transcripts with card details to a shared observability tool.
  • Shipping a fallback model nobody has evaluated.

🏆 Boss quiz

1. 1 M requests a month. The large model costs $0.02 per request, the small one $0.002. A cascade sends everything to the small model first and escalates 25% to the large one. Monthly cost?
Every request pays the small model: 1 M × $0.002 = $2,000. Escalations add 250,000 × $0.02 = $5,000. Total $7,000, versus $20,000 for large-only. $5,000 forgets that escalated requests also paid for the small call.
2. Gateway: 8 s timeout, 2 retries with 1 s and 2 s backoff, then a fallback that answers in 2 s. The primary hangs on every call. How long until the user gets an answer?
3 attempts × 8 s + 1 s + 2 s backoff + 2 s fallback = 29 s. Most users have given up. A circuit breaker (skip the dead primary) or a shorter timeout gets this to about 10 s or less.
3. A coding agent reads public GitHub issues, has the repo's deploy secrets in its environment, and can make HTTP requests. What's the most effective single change?
Issues are untrusted content; secrets are private data; HTTP is a way out. Removing the secrets and closing the network leg breaks the trifecta in code. Prompts and bigger models only lower the odds.
4. An HR product uses an LLM to recommend which employees get promoted, for EU customers. How does the EU AI Act (as amended in 2026) treat it?
Decisions on promotion and worker management are Annex III high-risk uses: risk management, data quality, logging, documentation, human oversight and accuracy requirements. The AI Omnibus moved those dates to 2 December 2027.
5. You send 100,000 full-HD screenshots (1920×1080) a day to Claude's standard tier, which downsizes them to 1,560 tokens each. At $3 per million input tokens, the daily image cost is…
100,000 × 1,560 = 156 M tokens × $3/M = $468 a day. Cropping to the relevant window, or a lower resolution when fine text isn't needed, cuts it proportionally.
